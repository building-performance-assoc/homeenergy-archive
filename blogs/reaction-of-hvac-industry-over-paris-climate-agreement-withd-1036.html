Reaction of HVAC Industry Over Paris Climate Agreement Withdrawal

By Rida Rizvi on behalf of Cielo Wigle · 25.07.2017

On 1st June, 2017 President Trump announced withdrawal from Paris Climate Agreement. This agreement took place between 196 countries to reduce greenhouse emissions. HVAC industry saw mixed reactions over this news.

What is Paris Climate Agreement?

Paris Climate Agreement is a non-binding covenant between 196 countries. End goal is to protect the environment from global warming and keep the temperature of earth below 2°C. Paris Climate Agreement sets target for nations to reduce greenhouse gas emissions yet there are no penalties in failing to do so.

Relation of Paris Climate Agreement to HVAC Industry?

US market has long been shifted to green initiatives and is considered pioneer is manufacturing and promoting smart green technology. Technology which has minimal impact on environment and human health is brain child of US market. Many leaders and influencers in industry believe this political decision will take the crown from US market and will give a major blow to economy as well.

Johnson Controls Inc. in a written statement said, “We’re very concerned this will harm the U.S. competitively from a technology standpoint.”

President of Air-Conditioning, Heating, and Refrigeration Institute (AHRI) issued statement following the withdrawal announcement expressing his concern and resolve for energy efficiency and commitment for environmental protection. He said,

 “The fact that the United States government has decided to seek alternative avenues other than the Paris Accord to address its energy and environmental objectives does not in any way alter the resolve of America's HVACR and water heating industry to reduce the environmental impact of the products and equipment we manufacture. Our industry has demonstrated its commitment to energy efficiency and environmental stewardship both domestically and abroad, which is evident in the global array of heating, cooling, water heating, and commercial refrigeration products that are more technologically advanced – while being more energy efficient – than ever before.” Stephen Yurek, AHRI President & CEO

Reaction of HVACR Industry:

HVACR Industry professionals are of mixed views and opinions over Paris Climate Agreement and announcement of President Trump to withdraw from it.

Many industry leaders were of the view that Paris Agreement has actually nothing to do with climate protection. People can alter their behavior to protect environment but mere equipment cannot control hazards. They blame public policy which according to them has misled consumers that technology or buying new equipment would result in energy efficiency and environmental protection.

“Improved energy efficiency is not an equipment issue; it’s a behavior issue. The longtime focus on the box is the behavioral issue that needs to be addressed if we want to make meaningful strides to reduce our carbon footprint.” Langston

James and Washam director of industry relations ACCA expressed their views in following statements;

“People who are concerned about their carbon footprints and want to make a difference should start in their own homes and consider whether their systems have been installed properly,” said Washam.

“The U.S. took it on the chin for this so-called deal,” said James. “We need to get our own house in order before we go signing agreements.”

Yet there are many contractors and manufactures who are worried about the impact on US market. Many jobs and currency would be in jeopardy because of this decision since US market was leading in clean technology. Like Johnson Controls Inc. issued their statement;

“We’re very concerned this will harm the U.S. competitively from a technology standpoint”

“We already saw what happened to our manufacturing in this industry when we did not participate in the Kyoto protocol. When the U.S. started turning to natural refrigerants, we had to turn to Europe and Japan because we were not under the protocol, we didn’t have the drive” said Keilly Witman, founder of NASRC and president of KW Refrigerant Management Strategy.

It has been observed that HVACR industry is reacting in all directions. Some are approving the withdrawal for Paris Climate Agreement while many are opposing. There are professionals who are choosing to take no sides and are agreeing to disagree.  One thing must be kept in mind that this is purely a political decision. Responses may vary yet resolve of HVACR industry is still the same- to sustain leadership in clean technology, green initiatives and environmental stewardship.
