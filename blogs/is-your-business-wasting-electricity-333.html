Is Your Business Wasting Electricity?

By Matthew Langham · 23.10.2012

Helping others save energy is a big part of our everyday lives and it is slowly becoming a cultural phenomenon in which we are saving the planet as well as our client's energy bills. But, do you know how energy efficient your office is?

Ensuring that your company is not wasting electricity covers a wide range of responsibilities, and making sure that lights are switched off at the end of the day is not the only consideration. Unsuitable or malfunctioning equipment could be wasting electricity or it could be hazardous.

The Importance of Choosing Suitable Equipment

Naturally you will want to reduce electricity costs, but unsuitable electrical equipment can be a false economy. Appliances which function perfectly well in a family home will be subjected to much greater usage in a commercial setting. For example, imagine a kettle. It will be in constant use, and as a result, is likely to wear out rapidly in a busy environment. Components such as the cabling could quickly become worn, creating a fire risk. Employees are busy, so they are less likely to take time to make sure they are using the equipment safely e.g. mopping up any spillage, ensuring the kettle is properly filled before use.

This is just one example of how using domestic equipment in an office setting could lead to electrical hazards in the workplace. Although the initial costs of providing a water heater may be higher, opting for a purpose-built appliance will prove to be a more cost-effective solution over time. After all, a fire caused by unsuitable equipment would be significantly more expensive for your organization.

Installing and Maintaining Your Equipment

Before installing any new equipment, you will need to ensure that you have a clear understanding of how it works, as well as any installation requirements. Equipment which has cabling requires careful consideration; naturally the cabling needs to be kept neatly, but also it needs to be clearly visible to avoid it becoming a trip hazard. Always bear in mind that your company could be liable for compensation if an employee has an accident at work. Keeping the cable visible also allows for regular inspection without any difficulty. You should also have a formally recorded maintenance schedule for all your equipment to be sure that everything is safely maintained. 

Getting your staff on board with health and safety issues is also important. It's likely that employees will make some use of company electricity, for example by charging mobile phones. The small cost to the company is likely to be offset by increased productivity from your staff. For example, an employee who is worried about a flat mobile phone battery for their journey home is bound to be less focused on their work. Staff dedication and morale is likely to be higher if they know they can charge their devices if they need to. However, PAT testing is a legal requirement for any equipment that uses a plug, and staff should be aware of this, but the issue could be solved by the provision of company phone chargers, or by ensuring that personal equipment is only charged via USB.

Disposing of Old Equipment

Every company should have a clear policy for disposing of redundant equipment, and you should also have a written inventory and maintenance schedule for all your equipment. Careful monitoring of these will alert you to any equipment which is getting old, making it more susceptible to electrical malfunction or unnecessary energy use. This enables forward-planning so that the purchase of any necessary replacement can be made in good time.
