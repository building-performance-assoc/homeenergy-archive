A Do-It-Yourself Home Energy Audit Checklist required for NYC Local law 87

By Julia · 25.05.2018

A do-it-yourself home energy audit may be the best tool to save on your utility bill by both increasing the energy efficiency of your home as well as improving your energy saving techniques. It is a simple procedure that does not require any expensive equipment or training but rather an attentive appraisal of problems that cause home energy losses. An energy audit checklist is a list of questions that will guide you through the process of identifying the areas that need improvement. By circling the appropriate answer you will be able to evaluate your home's energy efficiency in addition to your energy habits.

The energy audit checklist also required for local law 87 has nine sections. Each section focuses on one area of your home's energy performance.


• Air leakage: Areas where air leaks occur include windows, doors, light switches, electrical outlets, fans, basement, attic, fireplace and air-conditioning units. Since air leakage can dramatically increase your heating and cooling bills it is critical to check if all areas are correctly sealed


  • Insulation: Having a proper insulation is a great way to save on heating and cooling costs. Areas that need to be checked for insulation include attic, basement, walls, and floor.

  • Heating Systems: An efficient heating system could save you up to 30 percent on energy costs. A definite way to considerably decrease your heating expenses is to replace your aged heating system with an energy-efficient model.

  • Water heating: Water heating makes up 13 percent of your utility bill. Questions in this section will help you identify the energy efficiency of your water heating system including your showerhead and thermostat.

  • Windows and doors: Efficient windows can save you as much as 30 percent on cooling and heating costs. This section helps you assess the efficiency of your doors and windows.

  • Lighting: One of the simplest ways to save money on electricity is to switch from traditional incandescent to energy efficient fluorescent lights. In this section, you will appraise the energy performance of both indoor and outdoor lighting fixtures.

  • Appliances: Energy saving products and appliances use up to 20% less energy compared to traditional models. The energy efficiency checklist helps you assess the energy performance of your refrigerators, air-conditioners, washers, and ovens.

  • Computer/Electronics: In terms of energy efficiency, energy-saving computers and electronic devices outperform traditional models by 65%. The energy audit checklist helps you assess the energy efficiency of your computers, imaging equipment, audio/video equipment and television sets.

  • Habits: A meaningful home energy audit should also evaluate energy habits of the home's residents. By asking you to indicate whether you agree or disagree with a series of statements, this section will help you undertake a critical self-evaluation of your daily/seasonal energy habits.

Once your energy audit is completed, you will transfer your scores to the answer sheet and consider energy-saving measures. Start with low-cost improvements that can significantly reduce your energy bills. Other changes will require investments that will pay for themselves over time in saved energy. Should you decide to invest in energy efficient upgrades, make sure to check with your local utility about incentives available to help you pay for the costs of your upgrade measures.
