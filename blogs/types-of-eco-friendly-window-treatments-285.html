Types of Eco-Friendly Window Treatments

By Michael Proctor · 23.07.2012

Using eco-friendly window treatments isn’t only good for the environment. Saving energy also saves you money! In general, window coverings can help reduce energy consumption by providing insulation and thermal protection. In the winter months, window coverings provide insulation and reduce heat loss. In the warmer months, your window treatments help keep warm temperatures outside.  

While all window treatments can be helpful for saving energy, some are made especially with the environment in mind. These curtains, shades, blinds, etc. make use of recyclable materials, earth-friendly manufacturing, and products that help with energy efficiency.  

Eco-Friendly Drapes and Curtains
When shopping for new drapes or curtains, look for products that make use of natural materials that have a low impact on the environment. Companies using earth-friendly manufacturing standards produce less waste and byproducts. Some materials provide more energy efficiency than others, so be sure to check ratings for efficiency.  

Eco-Friendly Shades and Blinds
Blinds and shades keep out excessive sunlight, which, of course, cuts down on energy costs.  In addition to this, you can find products that are friendly to the environment. By purchasing blinds made with sustainable woods, you will help save the world’s forests and ecosystems. You can purchase shades which are made from recycled paper, which keeps waste out of our landfills. 

Questions to ask yourself while purchasing products:  

  • What’s the source of this product? Try to find products that use sustainable or recyclable materials.
  • Is this energy efficient? Learn how much the window treatment cuts down on energy use.  
  • Does this product release unhealthy fumes into the environment while being manufactured and/or once it’s in use? Some products, especially ones using certain plastics, can release toxic fumes into your home and cause bad air quality. Using natural products will help improve the air quality in your home. Some companies use better standards than others in their manufacturing processes. Check to see if the company making the window treatments you’re interested in use good practices that reduce harmful waste.  
  • How can I dispose of this product when I’m done with it? You might love your window treatment now, but what happens in a few years when you decide you’re ready for something new? Will you be able to recycle or repurpose the window treatment, or will it add to the amount of waste in our landfills? Use recyclable products whenever possible.
