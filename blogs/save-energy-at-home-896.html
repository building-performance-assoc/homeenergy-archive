Save Energy at Home

By Philip · 26.10.2016

Saving energy at Home

Electricity has been powering lights, appliances, and electronics without interruption for so long, that we almost take it for granted. As energy usage increases so do our energy bills. One of the prime motivations to save energy is to lower the energy bill. Besides energy conservation,help with saving non-renewable sources of energy like fossil fuels can, in turn, reduce pollution. Energy conservation starts in the home; here are a few ideas to begin this process.

Lighting

Replace traditional incandescent lights with energy-efficient lighting such as compact fluorescent lamps (CFLs), and light-emitting diodes (LEDs). Though they may cost a bit more initially, they will help save money in the long run by using less electricity. You can install motion sensors and timers to regulate the usage of light. Choose natural light over artificial during day-time. Daylight gives us the aesthetic effect. Additionally, it stimulates our mind and body. And do not forget the rule of thumb to switch off lights when not in use.

Laundry

Wash your clothes in cold water using HE detergents on the shortest cycle while using the lowest possible level of water. It would be a good idea to pre-soak the clothes to get rid of stubborn stains. Always wait until you have a full load of clothes. If possible (depending on the type of fabric) opt for a high-speed cycle of spinning and then hang the clothes on a clothesline rather than using the dryer.

Refrigeration

Move your fridge away from the sun, the heater and the stove to a cooler spot in the kitchen.  Maintain the fridge at between 3 and 5°C and your freezer at -18 °C to reduce your energy bills. Avoid placing hot food items into the fridge as it takes more energy to cool them. Make sure that the door seals close the fridge air tight without any leakage.

Windows

Install storm windows to reduce air leakage. You can also seal the gaps between the window frame and wall by caulking, weather-stripping and making use of window coverings. Other types of window treatments to save energy involves glazing – a process where double or triple glass window panes separated by a vacuum or gas filled space to reduce heat transfer across a part of the building envelope.

For the windows in warmer regions, arrange white blinds, drapes or curtains to reflect away the heat. Make sure to close the curtains during the day. Install awnings and sun-reflective films to decrease solar heat gain.

Landscaping

A well-designed landscape will remarkably reduce your heating and cooling costs while protecting your home both from the winter winds and the summer sun. A well-placed row of trees (deciduous ones), shrubs, and bushes provide cool shade and helps deflect wind. They block the sun during the summer while the bare branches let in solar radiation during winter.
