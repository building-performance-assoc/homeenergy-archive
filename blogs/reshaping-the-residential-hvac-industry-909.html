Reshaping The Residential HVAC Industry

By Steve Gadsby · 14.11.2016

A huge rift has been formed over the last decade reshaping the residential HVAC industry. Business practices, marketing, and cultures that created successful HVAC companies in the last century no longer apply. The companies that I’ve seen thriving have adopted a different culture and methods than the competitors. I used to be hesitant to share this information, but I've found over the years that other companies don’t listen to me anyway. (Even as we’ve grown 60% annually over a decade.)

There are a few key differentiators to those HVAC companies that outperform to those that have become stagnant in their revenue and declining in profit margins.

Successful HVAC Company Differentiators

1. Internet Marketing

A complete and total focus on internet related marketing (and abandoning all forms of non-digital). The list of sources for internet marketing is not exhaustive, but examples include Google Adwords, Facebook, and search engine optimization (SEO) As markets shift, I could see newer apps like Snapchat or Instagram becoming common marketing platforms for HVAC businesses.

2. A Sales Focused Culture

It’s easy for HVAC contractors to think that they are in a mechanical business or a service business. Unfortunately - they’re not. They are a sales and marketing company that happens to be working the HVAC space. With the focus being on sales and marketing and continuous improvement they’ll find more and more ways over the years to tweak marketing and other ways to get more and more leads.

In our business, we take our outside sales very seriously by tracking all leads and closing percentages plus we have regular role playing for sales improvement. New employees are thrown off a bit by our role playing, but we’d rather make the mistakes on the practice field than at game time.

We use a big scoreboard at our office to track only two things: sales calls created and jobs sold. It doesn't mean that everything else in our business isn't important. But without those two items working, we can't grow the business.

3. Problem Focused

It seems counter intuitive to focus on problems. But the truth is problems are the key to everything that an HVAC business needs. Your problems are screaming at you when it comes to what you need to fix in the company to go to the next level. As you grow, what worked last year probably won't work this year and you need to "level up." We have a spreadsheet listing all of our current problems (50 to 80 small and big issues), and we attack that list when things slow down.

Next Level Marketing and Lead Generation

In a nutshell, marketing in the HVAC industry is going to the next level - has been over the last decade. It's not enough anymore to put your name on the side of your van and experiment with different advertising types. The business will likely not make it - or have margins so poor you that you are working with virtually no profit. Over time, the companies that are adapting to the new reality are gobbling up market share while older companies that are not adapting are becoming inefficient, less profitable and fading away.

It’s also especially important to note that what worked last year for lead generation, probably doesn't work as well this year - the market is in flux. For us, Google Adwords targeting replacement leads has

always been an essential element. We need to keep a tight leash to make sure that ads are working - we can do this by monitoring conversion rates through Google Analytics. We are also finding success with Facebook ads - we’ve found it important to experiment with ads that catch people’s attention then increase the ad spend to support what is working.

The most important part of our marketing is search engine optimization (SEO). We would prefer not to pay for ads but would rather rank higher in search results organically. There are thousands of SEO companies but only a handful that are real professionals. SEO, more than any other profession, is in flux constantly and requires the SEO professional to be on top of every possible trend. I would suggest finding the best SEO you can find, start small and work with them to push the ball forward on your search engine results.
