How to Bid on HVAC Contracts

By Albert Krav · 01.04.2016

In many ways, entering a bid is the most important part of your job as an HVAC contractor. Without knowledge of how to make an accurate, competitive bid, you won’t be able to secure much work and the work you do get will wind up losing you money. Below are a few basic aspects of bidding that you can learn in order to maximize your earning potential and workload.

Quality Certifications

A potential customer is looking at a lot more than just the final numbers of your bid. They want to know about your work history, professional experience, accolades, and certifications. This information helps to establish your credibility and prove to potential customers that you can do quality work at the price you bid. Having certifications you’ve earned from trade associations or community organizations helps improve your credibility and standing as a contractor. They help convince your clients that you’re the right man for the job.

Pay Attention to Submission Guidelines

The majority of bids are submitted through government agencies and large private companies, which usually have a purchasing office. These organizations often have strict bid submission guidelines you must follow closely, or your bid will be discarded before getting a proper review. For example, you may be required to print out blueprints from a specific printer as part of your bid and if you don’t follow this seemingly unimportant guideline, your bid won’t be considered. Also, be sure to date and sign your bid or it may not be considered a valid, official bid.

Respect the Time Frame

Most larger companies have a section on their website concerning open bids. There you can find essential deadlines and updates related to your bid. A company may extend deadlines or bidding times if they’re awaiting other bids. Often a company holds a meeting before the bidding begins where they lay out all of their guidelines and deadlines. Adhering to these limitations may be your only chance to compete for the job. Tardiness makes your company look unprofessional and negatively affects your chances of winning the bid.

Make a Balanced Estimate

No matter how well you prepare your bid or follow the bidding guidelines, you may still lose the bid unless you present reasonable and finely calculated figures. Every project has phases, so make sure that you separate your bid into stages and include the cost of all materials and labor at each stage. Be sure to leave plenty of space in your timeline for delays, obtaining permits, and conducting inspections.

Another important aspect of a balanced estimate is having a contingency plan for possible cost overruns. Locate the most likely parts of the project that may send you over budget. Then, create a slight price range for your bid to pad your budget and demonstrate that you can handle unexpected issues. This means choosing your HVAC coil manufactuer wisely.

No matter how many companies are bidding on a job, having the proper certifications, following guidelines, and making financially sound estimates will make your bid more attractive and put you on the short list for those qualified to handle a job. Putting the utmost care into each of your bids will increase the likelihood that you will earn the bid and succeed in producing a quality job.
