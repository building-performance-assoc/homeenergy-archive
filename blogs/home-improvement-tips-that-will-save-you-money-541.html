Home improvement tips that will save you money

By xadilrenam · 12.03.2014

A new Scarborough Report has found that 56% of homeowners who invest money in home improvements spend over $1,000. The question is, how many of those costs could have been reduced by a little knowledge of easy things homeowners can fix themselves or ways you can avoid having to hire a contractor? A good bet is there are a lot of unnecessary bills being paid. Here are 3 ways to avoid them.

Check the showerhead

Shower heads aren't the most commonly thought of pieces of plumbing, but they can make a big difference in your shower experience. Older models that have been pumping out water for years slowly get caked with mineral deposits, especially in places with hard water. This lessens the water pressure and makes it more difficult to enjoy your warm, relaxing shower.

Instead of calling a plumber when your water pressure seems low, you can first try removing those mineral deposits by soaking the showerhead in a bowl of vinegar overnight and scrubbing it out with a grout brush the next day. You could also replace the head with a new one, perhaps a rain showerhead or one with a coiled arm. Who says you have to take the same, boring shower your whole life?

Increase storage space without renovation

Before you start looking for a storage unit or consider digging out your crawl space to make a basement or storage room, consider different ways to utilize your existing storage space. For instance, vacuum pack bags allow you to pack away seasonal clothing in less than half the space you'd normally need. You could also use spaces you wouldn't normally think of, like beneath your couch, the bed, or your dresser to store food storage so it isn't taking up room in your kitchen.

Protect your patio furniture

You may be finding as you check out your winter-weathered patio furniture that it's looking a little worse for the wear. A big problem with such furniture is rust buildup, and nobody wants to sit on cracked or moldy cushions. Rust removal is actually easy if you know this simple recipe: 2 tablespoons of lime or lemon juice mixed with 6 tablespoons of salt. Dab a
clean cloth in the scrub and rub it on any rusty spots you have. The rust will fall away, leaving you with like-new patio furniture. Cushions can also be easily replaced with newer, weather-resistant materials. You don't have to toss your whole set because the cushions have been worn out.
