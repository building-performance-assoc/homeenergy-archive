Go Green with a Wi-Fi Enabled Thermostat

Go Green!

We've all heard the term "go green." It's been the catch phrase of the environmentally conscious for many years. It means that by conserving natural resources and producing fewer pollutants, all of us can make our world, quite literally, a greener and healthier place for ourselves and for future generations as well. The best part about going green is that saving resources often translates into an economic savings, too. It's a win-win situation: With a little effort each day, we can have a healthier planet and more money in our pockets.

But how?

There are lots of easy ways to go green. Walking instead of driving, for instance, or carpooling when you really need to drive. Recycle as much as you can, too. Aluminum, plastics and newspapers are the obvious choices that pop into mind, but, donating gently used clothes and household items to a charity or thrift store is another less obvious way to help the planet. Items that are reused in this way don't end up in the landfill, and precious resources don't have to be expended making their replacements.

Lower the thermostat ... or raise it

Lowering the thermostat -- or raising it -- has always been a good way to save both money and the world's resources. Keeping your home a little warmer during the summer months and a little cooler in the winter saves on energy use. The U.S. Department of Energy website, energy.gov, recommends resetting your thermostat whenever you will be away from home and also when your family is sleeping. You can save, according to the website, as much as 15 percent on your yearly heating bill by simply lowering your thermostat 10 to 15 degrees for eight hours out of the day. If you're out of the house or sleeping under a cozy blanket for those eight hours, that's a pretty easy way to save money. The reverse is true, as well. In the summer time, raising the temperature of your home saves both money and energy.

And contrary to popular belief, you don't sacrifice your savings when your furnace needs to run a little longer to reheat the house later, or when you turn the air conditioning back on. Because your home was closer to the outdoor temperature during those times, energy loss is slowed and your savings are maximized.

Automated control

If you've decided to adjust your thermostat to save energy and money, you can, of course, do it the old fashioned way. You can simply raise or lower the setting as you leave the house or when you are headed to bed. In the past, you could also have used a programmable thermostat. This would make it easier to remember to change the settings. It could also help you make life a little more comfortable. You could, for example, warm up the house automatically just before your alarm clock goes off, or just before you're scheduled to come home from work. Some programmable thermostats will even self-correct for daylight savings time.

High tech options

There are now some high tech options available for managing the temperature in your home that go beyond the basic programmable thermostat. Some programmable thermostats are now Wi-Fi enabled. These options not only make it easier to raise and lower the temperature, but make monitoring your heating and cooling system more efficient and even a little fun.

Wi-Fi enabled thermostats are available from many well-known companies such as Lennox, Honeywell and GE, and can range in price from under $50 to over $200. The really cool thing about this new generation of thermostats is that they can be accessed remotely. This allows users to adjust their heating and cooling system from a computer, a smart phone or any device that has access to the Internet. You can, for example, turn the heat up before you even leave the office, if you happen to be coming home early. Some models even allow you to program the system to send you updates on needed maintenance or repairs.

No more excuses

Now that you can talk to your furnace or air conditioner anywhere and at any time, there are no more excuses for wasting energy dollars. With a Wi-Fi enabled thermostat, you can manage your home's heating and cooling to optimize both your family's comfort and your carbon footprint.
