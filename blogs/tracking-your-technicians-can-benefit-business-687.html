Tracking Your Technicians Can Benefit Business

By Macie Melendez · 28.04.2015

Customers don’t like waiting around for service—especially when it comes to home repairs. In their 2014 Customer Preferences for Residential Services report, Software Advice, a site helping businesses find dispatch software, identified the top pain points cited were lengthy arrival-time windows and having to wait for late technicians.

Scheduling, routing, and dispatching software with GPS-enabled capabilities can help overcome such pain points. Among other benefits, this functionality—which includes service dispatching, street-level routing, and smart scheduling—can help technicians plan routes more efficiently and provide more accurate arrival times.

For their report, Software Advice surveyed operators of home-service businesses currently using GPS-enabled dispatch software to learn what impact these solutions have had. To find the data in this report, they conducted a three-day online survey of seven questions, and gathered 8,169 responses from adults who have used residential services within the United States.

Technician Tracking

Software Advice asked how the use of various residential services software offerings would impact respondents’ likelihood to hire a residential services company. A majority (58%) said the use of technician tracking technology would “somewhat increase likelihood” (30%) or “greatly increase likelihood” (28%) to hire that company. This technology enables consumers to track the arrival time of technicians online, rather than having to rely on traditional “we’ll be there between noon and 6 p.m.” windows.

Effect of Software Features on Likelihood to Hire Service Provider

As you can see from the chart above, tracking the person who is coming to work on their home is a big issue for customers.

"Overall, home service companies need to consider the positive benefits of adopting software with customer-facing features. This is important to business processes, but also it can be a means of attracting more clientele. More specifically, it would be smart to target customer pain points that, truthfully, we now have the technology to fix. Tracking technicians is a perfect example of this," says Justin Guinn of Software Advice.

One beneficial technician-tracking capability field service software may offer, says Sam Pillar, CEO and co-founder at Jobber is geofencing: the same technology utilized by popular rideshare and taxi service Uber. This technology acts quite literally like a digital “fence,” enabling businesses to set boundaries for service, create alerts upon entry and exit to and from these boundaries, and automatically notify customers when their technician is nearby.

As Pillar explains, “If you're scheduled to do a job at 2 p.m., but at five minutes to 2 p.m., you’re still 10 miles away, you’d be able to automatically send a text message to a customer saying you’re going to be a little late."
