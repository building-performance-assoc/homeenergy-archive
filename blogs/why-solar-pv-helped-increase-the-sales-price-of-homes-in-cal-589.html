Why Solar PV Helped Increase the Sales Price of Homes in California?

By Julia Young · 08.08.2014

Installing solar panels on the roof of your home may not be such a bad investment idea after all. A study conducted by the Department of Energy, Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory (LBNL) has found homes that have solar panels command higher resale value.

The Findings of the Study

The study was conducted between 2000 and 2009 in 31 counties in California, covering more than 72,000 homes. It may be noted that California commands the top position in the ranking of states with largest solar power capacities. Several factors influencing the sale, such as age of the house, size of the house, neighborhood of the house and the market fluctuations, were controlled for the study.

Researchers found homes that had solar PV panels often sold for higher prices compared to homes that did not have solar panels. Furthermore, it was observed homes with solar panels saw solar premium range from $3.9 to $6.4 for each installed watt on an average. However, researchers also found the premium reduces as the solar systems get old.

The Reason for the Hike

Researchers opine the increase in resale values of homes with solar panels is because the carrying cost of the houses reduces. Hence, home buyers are more than willing to pay more for the houses. Researchers reached this conclusion since homes with solar PV systems would save money on their electricity bills and the savings could be used by homeowners to improve their homes.

The Findings Coming to the Aid of Californian Homeowners

Researchers from LBNL found that there was an issue valuing homes that had installed solar systems. While older studies had similar results like the LBNL study, most home appraisers did not give homes a higher value if they had solar systems. There were some appraisers that considered solar PV systems and assigned higher values to homes, but this valuation was usually not backed by the right data.

Hence, the LBNL study proved to be a boon to homeowners. It took into account sales data of 1,894 homes with solar systems and 70,425 homes without solar systems sold throughout California between 2000 and 2009. Researchers took into consideration three factors, which are as follows: 

  • Regression analysis of home sale prices over a period of time
  • Estimated solar premiums based on the age and size of the solar system
  • Comparing actual solar premiums to the estimate

It was found 1 kW increase in the solar system size added approximately $5,911 to the resale value of the house. The study also found that for each year of the solar PV system, the premiums reduced by 9% because people have a misconception that the system’s value and output reduces with age. This led researchers to believe most home buyers view solar systems like electronics, which become less attractive with age even if electronics work as well as a new model.

The LBNL study is a boost to the already growing solar industry. Previously, homeowners view solar PV systems as a way to help the environment. However, it now appears, homeowners have realized that a solar electric system is an investment. California has approximately 100,000 solar systems installed across the country and over 90% of these individual systems are installed in homes. Today, not just homeowners but even builders are contemplating installing solar PV systems because of the benefits these systems bring with them.
