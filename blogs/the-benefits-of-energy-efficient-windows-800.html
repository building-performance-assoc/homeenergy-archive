The benefits of energy-efficient windows

By Andrew Gillespie · 22.01.2016

Saving energy makes sense. Whether you're looking to save a few quid on your bills, keep your home toasty through the winter or just simply save the world, there's always reason to conserve energy.

 

Having energy-efficient windows in your home, whether they be double or triple-glazed, has its advantages. Most significantly, energy-efficient windows reduce energy costs. Reduced air leakage means that a lot of the heating you pay for from your energy supplier isn't lost out the window, which in turn means less energy is required to heat your home during those chilly winter months. Studies suggest that in cool climates, energy saving windows can reduce your heating bill by up to 31%, depending on the condition and type of window. They are constructed to eliminate drafts that occur when the cold glass pane of the window cools the air around it that drops to create this feeling of draftiness. This movement pattern contributes significantly to heat loss.

 

And energy-efficient windows also reduce moisture in the air and condensation. The low-e glass used has a microscopic layer of transparent metal on its face, much thinner than that of even a human hair, that repels long-wave infrared energy. This type of glass also repels condensation, meaning no build-up of moisture and no frost forming about the windows. No need to turn the heating up, then.

 

Energy-efficient windows also go some way to blocking out the harmful rays of the sun. The coatings on the glass help to reduce ultraviolet rays by almost 75%. This reduces sun damage significantly, mainly regarding furniture like sofas, carpets and artwork. But curiously, despite the blocking of the suns' rays, the windows do not require tinting in order to work effectively and therefore do not reduce visible light whatsoever. Let there be light!

 

Ultimately, energy-efficient windows are proven to do exactly as they should - save energy. Improving energy efficiency means a reduction in greenhouse gases that harm the planet and contribute to climate change. Additionally, any savings in energy you do make translate perfectly back into your wallet. So, whichever way you look at it, saving energy makes sense.
