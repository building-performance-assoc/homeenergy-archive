How To Sell Homeowners On Green Building Standards For Residential Properties

By Kristina Frunze · 28.01.2020

While reading this post by one of my fellow contributors on this site, I resonated with its acknowledgment of a major challenge: convincing people to consider green building residential strategies. Whether you’re an HVAC professional or someone designing homes as a whole, you’ve probably encountered this. 

While my fellow contributor’s piece focused on real estate agents, here are some brief tips for selling homeowners on practical solutions as well.

Tip #1. Green Residential Building Design Can Save Homeowners Money

Residential green building materials have come quite a long way. Public perception hasn’t always kept up, however. Many people have an outdated view that equates sustainability to a costly compromise.

In reality, technology like water filters can save homeowners thousands of dollars per year while also reducing the amount of plastic that ends up in landfills. Even someone who is not environmentally-conscious can’t argue with the numbers in this regard, considering the savings.

Updated sprinkler systems, efficient thermostats, and new water appliances also offer significant savings that can’t be argued with. This is why countering resistance with financially-sound arguments works so well; there are so many good examples.

Tip #2. Green Building Home Design Can Help Secure A Property Investment

Depending on where your prospective client’s home is located, embracing green building residential construction practices can mean the difference between still having a home in 20 years or not.

Experts predict that coastal communities in the United States face increased flooding risk due to climate change. It’s not fear-mongering; it’s a very real threat exacerbated by corrosion. With the increasing economic viability of residential green sustainable building materials, there’s little excuse for ignoring new and better techniques.

Even investments in non-waterfront homes can benefit from green practices. At some point, clients will sell their homes -- likely to a younger generation that overwhelmingly prioritizes sustainability. Properties lacking green technology may fare worse in the marketplace.

Tip #3. Regulations Are Increasing

The principles of green residential building design are being forced into practice by increasing government regulations. I discussed some of these regulations as they pertain to the HVAC industry here.

In some cases, regulations do not require immediate action but rather give homeowners time to implement changes over a longer rollout period. Financially savvy homeowners should understand that waiting until changes are absolutely necessary is not the best option. 

One would be wiser to get ahead of the curve and begin implementing green building home design practices like efficient water heaters and air conditioners. In some cases, early adopters may be able to claim rebates.

Tip #4. Sustainability Often Enhances Community

Green building residential construction practices often enhance the community experience through more obviously-shared energy sources and materials. 

A classic example is the many net-zero communities that have sprung up around the United States. Houses in these communities use the same residential green building materials, look alike, and are often configured in a way that encourages interaction.

Humans have an innate psychological need to be part of a community, which gives this line of reasoning a universal appeal. 

People who already have a sense of uncertainty concerning the environment tend to respond well to this line of reasoning as well. It’s well known among marketers that people experiencing fear long for a community even more than usual.

Of course, you should never use fear to maliciously manipulate potential clients into buying your products or services. When you use this tip, all statements should be based on facts and discussed with a level head.

Tip #5. Residential Green Building Materials Are In Style -- And Will Likely Remain That Way

The environment has been an international concern for decades. It’s highly unlikely this will change. Even absent from the environmental movement, sustainable architecture at all scales is only becoming more popular. Natural materials look better and never go out of style, which can help homeowners craft a property that looks as great 10 years from now as it does today.

Statistics from the World Resources Institute show that the demand for green building is also on the rise. Only 5% of commercial buildings in 2005 qualified for LEED or Energy Star certification. Just over 10 years later, in 2017, that number rose to 38%.
