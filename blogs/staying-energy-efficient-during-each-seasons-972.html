Staying Energy Efficient During Each Seasons

By James Neff · 07.03.2017

With the transition from winter to spring and higher temperatures approaching, there are a number of ways consumers can make their home energy efficient. Most of these tips are free, and the rest remain relatively inexpensive when compared to the energy savings you’ll experience on your utilities.

Conduct an Energy Audit

The first step any business or individual can take is to conduct an energy audit on your commercial or residential building. Depending on your home’s size, an energy audit can cost anywhere between $300-$500, according to the Department of Energy, but it can save multitudes more. It's generally a four-hour assessment that  identify areas of your home where energy efficient practices and equipment can be instituted to save you money and reduce energy waste. According to the Department of Energy, individuals, who qualify, can receive up to $1,500 in tax credits to perform an energy audit, and many power companies offer free energy audits.

Businesses will benefit by placing smart meters within their commercial units, and using an energy tracker to identify areas of energy waste. These tools allow businesses and individuals to monitor energy usage, and adjust their budget accordingly to reduce energy costs.

Install a Programmable Thermostat

No matter what season, the temperature of your thermostat will generally determine your home heating and cooling costs. A programmable thermostat allows you to maintain different temperature settings based on when you're at your residence or the temperature outside, and it can be adjusted between nighttime and daytime settings. It's estimated that you could save up to 10% of your heating and cooling costs by simply turning your thermostat 7-10 degrees back from its normal setting.

Home Lighting

For year-round savings, using incandescent bulbs can seriously injure your energy budget. A 23-30 watt energy efficient bulb can produce the same amount of light as a 100 watt incandescent bulb. Replace outdated bulbs with CFL or LED lights for energy savings. CFL and LED bulbs produce 75% less heat than incandescent bulbs.

Winter and Fall Tips

Fix Leaks

Leaks within your home can cause air to escape, overworking your HVAC system. Identify areas outside of your home where brick and siding meet that could be causing unnecessary air to seep in. Within your home, inspect areas in the basement, attic, or around vents that could be causing a draft. Seal any leaks with caulk, spray foam, or weather stripping. Also, insulate any ducts that may be letting in air. Sealing leaks can make your heating system more energy efficient and can save up to 20% on your utility bill annually.

Update Old Windows

Windows could be the biggest source of draft within your house. Seal window frames with a heavy-duty plastic sheet to secure them in place. Place plastic film on the inside of your window frame and pat it tightly to make sure no air can get through. Consider placing drapes or shades over your windows to further reduce any airflow into the house. Depending on your climate, it may be ideal to open southern facing windows during the winter months to allow natural sunlight to heat those rooms.

Maintain Heating Systems

Water heaters account for 18% of the energy consumed residential homes. Reducing your temperature setting to 120 degrees will reduce energy costs significantly. It's recommended that users replace furnace filters on their furnace at least once a month. If you are using a wood or pellet heating system, be sure to clean the flue vent monthly and to apply a wire-brush to the inside of the fixture to ensure your house is being heated efficiently.

Summer and Spring Tips

Utilize Ceiling Fans

Ceiling fans can be an excellent cooling source that will allow you to reduce your A/C by 4 degrees without reducing comfort. Be sure to use energy-star ceiling fans and to turn off fans when not in the room, as this will end up raising your energy costs. Placing ventilation fans within your bathroom or kitchen to reduce steam and moisture will cool your house and reduce A/C runtime.

Maintain HVAC Equipment

It's recommended that users check A/C air filters once a month and clean them of any dirt that may be affecting their performance. Be sure to schedule regular maintenance for your cooling systems to avoid major repairs.

Air condensers, evaporator coils, and heat pumps can break down over time and can become a costly repair. Just because many of these parts are made with silver doesn’t mean you want to spend any more coin fixing them up than you have to. Regular A/C maintenance will also ensure peak performance during the summer months and maximum energy efficiency. Lastly, be sure to keep any lamps or electronic equipment away from A/Cs as they will sense heat from these sources and run longer as a result.

There are many ways to maintain energy efficiency in any climate all year round. In a world where even muscle cars are starting to come out with hybrids, these small steps can make a huge difference without breaking the bank or taking up too much of your time.
