Top 3 Generators for Home Use

By Ashley Haigh · 06.06.2013

Top 3 Standby Generators for Homes

Americans rely heavily on electricity to heat and cool their homes, store their food, and stay connected with their family and friends. Even seemingly mundane activities like charging a mobile phone or making a  cup of coffee are impossible without electricity. Anytime a home loses power people are forced to return to the dark ages, literally. That is why having a backup power supply is so important. Whether you live in an area susceptible to strong storms or not, from New York to Houston home generators have proven to be a valuable, sometimes life-saving asset should a powerful storm or power grid failure occur.  Hurricanes, tropical storms, blizzards, and infrastructure failures can sever power for extended periods of time. Home generators provide reliable backup energy for your family and irreplaceable peace of mind.

When buying a generator, there are a few different things that you need to consider. First of all, the type of generator is important. Portable and Standby generators are the two most used types of generators when it comes to supplying energy for residential homes.

Portable generators allow you to pick up and move the generator, perhaps to a remote area in order to power an event such as a camping trip. Their purpose is mainly recreational.

Standby generators are stationary and hard-wired into homes,  usually providing a substantial amount of power ranging anywhere between 5,000 and 25,000 watts. These generators are best for supplying an entire house with power during a power-outage, as they provide much more power than portable generators. Most standby generators are set to power on automatically in the event of a power outage.

Most people are unfamiliar with the wattage system, but it’s important to know the basics when it comes to powering your home and generator shopping . Watts are actually a number that represents  the rate of joules per second – so we are technically calculating the rate at which our houses burn through energy.  

Average Watts Used to Power a Home

The average house uses approximately 1.5 Kw (1500 watts) an hour, so you’re generally going to want to find a generator that can supply this type of power at minimum. Adjust energy consumption estimates based on the square footage  of your house. There is no one-fits-all answer to how much energy your house uses. Again, standby generators are the most reliable backup power source when it comes to supplying energy to a home, so start browsing those models in particular.

Transfer Switches and Panels: Another thing people may not understand when buying a generator, is transfer switches and panels. These turn the generator on and off, and can be either manual or automatic.

Manual transfer switches are used more with portable generators because they require the user to be home during a power outage in order to turn the generator on, but are sometimes used for standby generators as well.

Automatic transfer switches are used with standby generators and automatically turn the generator on when they sense a decrease in line voltage. Automatic transfer switches also turn the generator off when they sense the line power has been restored. These are most always used with standby generators and are handy in providing whole home backup power when an outage occurs and you are not at home.
