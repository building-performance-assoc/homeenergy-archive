Book Review&mdash;Green Building Illustrated

By Jason Henderson · 15.06.2015

For those not familiar with “Ching books”, they’re essentially the gold standard for visual architecture reference and aid in the design process. Architect Francis D.K. Ching’s series of textbook-size volumes go through conceptual to intimate detail of design and construction for both novice and professionals alike. They’re commonly noted as the best and easiest-to-understand references for design professionals and builders the world over. It would be rare to find an architect’s shelf without several of them.

In this latest book, Green Building Illustrated, Ching teamed-up with Ian Shapiro, founder of TAITEM Engineering (Technology As If The Earth Mattered), an Ithaca, New York-based engineering firm with an international practice presence, and one that conducts in-house research, which has further broadened the area of knowledge for green build and retrofit projects. Shapiro brings decades of experience working in the green building industry.

The Book is Straightforward & Well-Structured

Shapiro provides the explanations alongside Ching’s drawings of each topic. The chapters go from an introduction and first principles, then codes, siting, shape, envelopes, thermal zoning, utilities, renewables, materials, and wraps-up with a segment on quality in design and construction. The book is separated into 19 chapters, with hundreds of topics, from surface area configurations to thermal bridging and rainwater harvesting.

It does superb job at answering the countless “how does that work?” and “what should I be considering?” questions inherent in any green building topic. For example, in the Outer Envelope chapter, the section on walls shows each wall assembly, several cross-sections, and then delves into the thermal considerations of each assembly by pointing-out key factors that determine the efficiency of each system, such as where thermal conduction, bridging, and seal breaks commonly occur or may be prevented with the proper approach.

Fit for the Pros Too: Learn Something New

As with many Ching books, not only is it accessible to non-design folks, but seasoned pros should also find some further illumination. Detailed tables show the reflectance values of various interior finishes, which can significantly reduce the need for artificial lighting, and the materials chapter provides a variety of material reduction and reuse techniques. Since the book is geared towards nearly all building types, both commercial and residential, it stands as a useful resource for designers and builders of all forms. The book is loaded with rules of thumb: lengths, amounts, timing, and so on, within each topic that would typically require a deep set of knowledge or experience in design and construction to master.

Bottom Line: A Must-Have for Understanding the Concepts & Best Practices

For the price, this book is a bargain considering the cost of a mistaken design or building approach for a single home, and undoubtedly for a larger commercial project. Even for those without the time to read through it entirely, this book is well-worth having on the shelf to refer to during projects of all types, and for those new to the topic and looking for a comprehensive knowledge-base in building green, this is a most-read.

 

Jason Henderson is a Master's candidate at Cornell's Baker Program in Real Estate.
