Using Lighting to Improve Home Energy Performance

By Leigh Durrant · 21.11.2016

Most considerations over home energy performance center around heating. Whether it is keeping a home warm in winter or cool in summer, technology is being employed to circulate air, insulate walls, floors, and ceilings. When building our new home, we wanted these things too - a passive house which could generate electricity rather than consume it. A carbon neutral, environmentally friendly home is the dream of many; especially those who can afford it. But we noticed, one element was consistently being forgotten by experts - lighting.

Light bulbs are one of the major consumers of electricity after heating and appliances yet they are often forgotten. Lowering this cost while having a brighter home was vital to our plans. A good part of this revolved around maximizing natural light whether it was through the use of skylights, large windows, or clever painting, we knew we could only do so much with nature; especially given how dark winters can be around these parts.

Comparing Different Bulb Types

This required us to check different types of bulbs. At first we were comparing downlights with spotlights, tubes with strips, security lights with bulkheads. Many families compare single lights with diffuse lighting options - more bulbs, more coverage. But then we realized there were many types of bulbs within this - CFL, halogen, LED, and incandescent. So how do these compare?

In terms of pure energy efficiency, LEDs are by far the best. They use one-tenth of the electricity of a traditional incandescent bulb, a third of that of a halogen bulb, and half of that of a fluorescent or CFL bulb. Furthermore, they produce less heat than rival bulbs, which means both energy efficiency and greater flexibility to light your home. We went with this product and found them useful, easy to install, more hardy than other bulbs, and our energy efficiency has shot up.

Learning from Street Light Failures

The use of LEDs in street lights is coming under attack across the U.S. and other countries too such as the UK and Canada. There are two main reasons for this and they are worth considering and applying to home lighting too:

  1. Blue Lighting: Many street lights use white light LEDs which actually give off a bluish glow. These have been linked to sleep and body clock disruption and associated ill effects as well as complaints of increased glare from drivers.
  2. Authorities Not Saving Money: One of the major points, as I’ve mentioned above, is that LEDs save money, but not for local authorities. It seems time and time again rather than saving the money, authorities are using the spare budget on making more lights, which is worsening night glows, disruption to wildlife, and so on.

Solutions for home lighting are simple. When lighting our new home we made sure to buy LEDs with higher amounts of infrared. They give of more of a typical yellow glow than the harsh street lights, which means they do not disrupt sleeping patterns or body clocks. Secondly, the point was to cut electricity bills with energy efficiency, so we used the perfect amount of lights around the home rather than fill in the gaps with more and more. Light design is essential to the light vs cost balance.
