5 REASONS SOLAR POWER IS EXPLODING IN POPULARITY

By Jessica Reynolds · 12.02.2016

At the start of 2016, following on from the landmark Climate Change Conference in Paris, there can be fewer of us than even who are not aware of the urgent need to use these clean power sources. There are several forms of alternative or green energy that most of us are familiar with.

However, the one that seems to have recently gained popularity and acceptance at the fastest rate is solar power. This is the case with individuals, companies, and in both developed and developing countries. There are a multitude of reasons for this popularity but arguably the main ones are: 

  1. Accessibility: Although there are certainly countries that don’t get a whole lot of sun, most of the global population lives within the “sunbelt” which lies within 24o of the Equator. This means that not only is solar energy the most abundant form of energy but it also results in the reality that most countries are able to harness the extraordinary power of the sun. 

In addition, the rapid growth in the solar power industry means that there is an ever-growing network of suppliers and installers and consumers can select a supplier that best suits their needs, location, and budget as there are now leasing options in addition to purchase options.

  1. Dependability: Like many other technologies, solar power module design has its roots in space technology. This means that the technology involved has been designed to be very durable and low maintenance. This is in large part to the materials used and the fact that they have no moving parts. 

Thanks to their design, the lifespan of solar panels is now estimated to be about 25 years. This has both practical and financial advantages.

  1. Ecological benefits: For many individuals and businesses reducing our impact on the environment and the amount of fossil fuels used and carbon generated is important. Solar power offers significant environmental benefits.

 While the manufacturing process of solar panels does incur a “carbon debt” or produce some carbon, solar energy repays the debt within a couple of years. Thereafter panels produce no emissions while they generate power and any toxic emissions are massively less than those produced by coal.

Finally, many panels can be recycled or used in some other energy generating system when they reach the end of their life. This, too, reduces the use of fossil fuels and the production of carbon dioxide (CO2) and other pollutants and greenhouse gasses.   

  1. Financial savings: For most of us this consideration is no small thing as the cost of living has increased dramatically and our incomes don’t keep pace with inflation. Using solar power can reduce energy bills significantly. It can also add to the market value of a property.

Let’s not kid ourselves though: the initial installation can be expensive. However, this is changing. Apparently a solar panel costs roughly 1% of what it did 3 decades ago. This now means that solar power more cost-effective than retail electricity in many parts of the US and the world. 

What further eases the installation burden is the availability of incentives and rebates that governments are increasingly offering. In some areas there are also tax benefits. Finally, within three to seven years a solar power installation has paid for itself.

In some areas there is the facility to feed excess power back into the grid. The home owner or business that does this then receives a credit so if, for some reason, they must draw from the grid the accumulated credit will reduce the cost. If you’d like to learn more about state-specific solar panel information, you can check out solaractionalliance.org.

  1. Energy independence: The cost of retail electricity is increasing globally and many of us are battling to pay high utility bills. Signs are that this won’t change any time soon. The alternative is to produce enough power to be independent of the power grid.

 This kind of independence doesn’t only apply at an individual or corporate level. Countries also benefit as they become less reliant on other nations to supply oil or other fossil fuels.

While there’s no denying that further cost reductions on components and technological developments such as improved power storage capabilities are needed, we also can’t get round the fact that solar power already offers a range of truly significant benefits for individuals, communities, corporates, and nations.
