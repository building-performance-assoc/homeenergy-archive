Redefining the American Dream

By Macie Melendez · 13.10.2014

Recently, I attended a panel on the future of affordable housing. While the focus of the panel was on the idea of “home”—something that the organization that created the event, Home Matters, knows a lot about—panelists also discussed ways in which the home has a ripple effect on our society.

Lisa Gelfand, a member of the panel as well as founding and managing principal of Gelfand Partners Architects in San Francisco, said that a home offers “a true feeling of belonging, safety, and security.” It’s not simply a roof and four walls, but rather a place to feel comfortable, safe, and empowered to take on the outside world. “People have to come home and they have to feel like this place matters,” she said.

A home is a common part of what is often referred to as the American Dream. A house, a family, and a job are all part of our idea of a successful life. But in a country where buying a home is not affordable to many, how do we change our definition of the American Dream to become more attainable?

Dave Brown, CEO of Home Matters, a movement focused on raising awareness and funds to make home a reality for everyone, took the time to help me answer that question and others. Following is our conversation.

Q&A with Dave Brown

Macie Melendez: You mentioned during the panel that Home Matters is redefining the American Dream. What does that definition look like in the real world?

Dave Brown: The pursuit of the American Dream is now broader than ever. It reflects new and changing American values. Having a physical structure isn’t enough anymore, and based on who you are and where you are in life, ‘home’ can mean many things. We need to redefine the American Dream to reflect those changing and new perceptions. For Millenials, home might mean a small, temporary space with access to transportation and jobs. For baby boomers, it might mean downsizing into a home that fits their needs and mobility. For young families, it might mean access to good schools. So, Home Matters believes the new American Dream is one where every American lives in a safe, nurturing environment with access to quality health care, good schools, public spaces and community services. We believe the New American Dream is a societal commitment to improve and balance quality of life and that home is our nation’s common denominator. 

MM: What do you think are the top 3 benefits for someone having an affordable place to call home?

DB: One out of two Americans has had to make at least one major sacrifice in order to pay their rent or mortgage. Therefore, having an affordable home brings a significant benefit: not having the stress of figuring out how to pay your rent or mortgage or what you’re sacrificing in order to pay your rent or mortgage. In addition, Home Matters is making the case, with support from the MacArthur Foundation, that a safe, healthy home brings all sorts of other benefits, like lower obesity, better health outcomes for children, more educational opportunities, and an overall better quality of life. 

MM: I asked the panelists about energy efficiency's role in building affordable housing. Do you have any thoughts on how energy efficiency measures could be incorporated into this idea of the new American Dream?

DB: Energy efficiency speaks directly to affordability. Any money not spent on housing costs is money available for other things. Housing costs have become such a heavy expense burden that the quality of life for families and individuals suffers as a result. Having an energy efficient home that can lower costs can ultimately assist in creating the home environment that brings a high quality of life.

MM: You said that we're in the midst of a housing crisis. Can you elaborate on that?

DB: Yes. In addition to the aforementioned statistic that 1 out of 2 Americans has had to make at least one major sacrifice in order to pay their rent or mortgage, there are also half a million people on the streets, one-fourth of which are children. Home Matters is creating public awareness that we as a country need to change the way we think about where we live. Home is not just about the four walls around us, it’s about the environment around us that affects the choices we make in life and who become. We exist to raise funds and build awareness of the need for more affordable homes and better communities across the country.
