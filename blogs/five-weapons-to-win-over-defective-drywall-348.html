Five Weapons to Win Over Defective Drywall

By Humza Hemani · 21.11.2012

If your house was made from around 2004 to 2007, or if there's been repair or remodeling work involving new drywall, there's a chance that that work could have been done using defective drywall. During the housing boom from 2004 to 2007, there was a shortage of drywall, so quite a large amount of it had to be imported. Normally, during shortages, drywall is imported from Canada or Mexico, but this shortage was so large that the USA looked elsewhere. China ended up supplying most of the shortage, over 550 million pounds of drywall. Unfortunately, a lot of this drywall has turned out to release gases that can ruin the wiring, tubing, and, most importantly, your health.

So how do you know whether the drywall you imported was tainted or not? Luckily there some great ways to figure that out. Here five tools you can use to see if your drywall has been hurting you and everything else in your house.

Your nose:That's right. Smell your house. If you can smell something like rotten eggs, but can't find any eggs, you've got sulfur gases in your air, which is the main gas that defective drywall releases. You might not notice the smell because you've gotten used to it, so try spending a few hours out of your house and taking a nice, big sniff when you come back.

Your medical history: The gases that defective drywall can release can hurt your health, like I mentioned earlier. Your respiratory system is the most likely to be effected. Chronic coughing, sinus problems, headaches, rashes and even asthma attacks can be caused by the gases. Look through your and your family's medical history. If you notice that you've had more of these or related problems since moving into your house, bad drywall could be the culprit.

A Light Bulb: The gases that defective drywall releases aren't just bad for your health. They can also corrode different metals, especially copper. Normal copper corrosion causes copper turn either reddish or greenish, but corrosion caused by gases from bad drywall leaves the copper black and ash-like on the outside. As a result of this corrosion, electrical systems can start to act strangely, potentially causing serious damage. If you've noticed that light bulbs have been burning out more quickly than normal, it could be because gases from tainted drywall have been corroding your wiring.

Your Air Conditioner: It's not just wiring that can go bad. Metal components in different appliances can be ruined by those gases, as well. Air conditioners are particularly vulnerable to this, because they have air flowing through them constantly. If you've had to replace or repair your air conditioning system after less time than normal, it could be from gases from drywall. Inspect the coils of the conditioner, as they're likely to be copper. If they've turned an ashy black, rather than red or green, it's a pretty good sign that your drywall is bad.

A Silver Spoon: Now, this is an interesting test. Silver is  an unreactive metal, so it doesn't corrode at all under normal circumstances. However, the gases the bad drywall release do corrode silver. Try hanging a silver spoon (or something else that's silver) in front of an air conditioner, or somewhere else where air will be flowing past it. After a few days, if the spoon gets tarnished, you've got some bad drywall.

These tests should let you know whether there are gases being released by bad drywall. Remember that there could be drywall in your home that isn't actually releasing any gases because of environmental factors, like low humidity. Also keep in mind that you could have drywall In your home from multiple sources, of which only some is bad.
