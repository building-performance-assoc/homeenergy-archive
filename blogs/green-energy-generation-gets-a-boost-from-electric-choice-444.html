Green Energy Generation Gets a Boost From Electric Choice

By Rachelle · 05.07.2013

The game playing changes that have been brought about by the deregulation of the energy industry has created its own happy side-effect. Consumers who are environmentally conscious now have the opportunity to support their passion by choosing an electricity supplier whose energy supplying practices are ecologically sustainable. Here are some things you should know about the different ways electricity is generated, and the varying impacts they have on the environment.

Green and Brown Electricity

Both types of electricity are produced from natural resources, but the difference lies in how the energy is generated from these sources, and the effects on the environment. Green energy is generated from natural resources such as biomass, wind and solar energy. Brown electricity is generated from resources such as coal, natural gas and fossil fuels. The strategies used for green energy production makes use of the energy that is naturally given off by the green resources, while those used for brown energy production makes use of the energy released by the brown resources during the process of burning. The electricity experts at TXU Energy spend quality time helping their clients find a supplier that offers them the option of selecting a plan that offers one form of energy or the other or a combination. Let us take a further look at the generating process for each type of energy.

Green Energy

Some electricity suppliers generate their energy from 100 percent renewable resources, and some do so using combined heat and power (CHP). Green energy becomes available to consumers when the energy supplier uploads it to the grid. Consumers who want to support green energy production and help save the environment can then opt to purchase a green plan that will allow them to be supplied with the energy of their choice (wind, solar or biomass). Hydroelectricity involves the capturing of energy from moving water. The water can come from a flowing source such as a river, or from a dam where the water is held and caused to move when it is forced through a hydraulic turbine that is connected to a generator. Solar energy is generated when solar panels are used to capture and store energy from the sun. There are no wastes that re-enter the environment and cause harm.

Brown Energy

Brown resources yield a higher density of energy because they are like a compact source of carbon. When resources such as fossil fuels (oil, gas and coal) are burnt, they return high volumes of carbon dioxide to the atmosphere.This contributes to global warming. The generation of brown energy from these resources is therefore harmful to the environment. We see and feel the side effects which include air pollution and acid rain.

When we opt to purchase green energy we are supporting the environment as one less customer using brown energy means there is less demand for it. If the demand for brown energy decreases then the practices of burning fossil fuels and natural gas for energy would also decrease. How much you pay for your green energy will depend on your supplier as they set their own rate, but do not expect it to automatically be cheaper than brown energy. If the supplier offers discounted green energy the cost will be lower than that of brown energy. If it not discounted it will still even out as you will not be liable for a Climate Change Levy when you use green energy.
