2015 Green Building Trends

By Monica Gomez · 13.05.2015

For both residential and commercial structures, owners are embracing green innovations for a variety of reasons. Concern for the environment is one of them. Other motivations to use green building approaches include saving money and protecting health. That's why the biggest green trends in 2015 will be those that promise long-term savings and an improved indoor air quality.

Going Natural

Natural building materials such as stone and lumber have always been practical and popular, and you can expect them to be even more in demand this year, reports Digital Journal. Materials that emit none or low limits of volatile organic compounds (VOCs) improve indoor air quality and are especially important for families with children and individuals with asthma or chemical sensitivities. “Living roofs” are estimated to be in high demand for both residential and commercial buildings. A roof covered in living greenery protects the building from runoff and protects inhabitants from daily radiation exposure.

Net Zero Energy

A major focus in 2015 will be on constructing buildings that produce as much renewable energy as they use: zero-energy buildings. Photovoltaic cells that convert solar energy into direct current energy will play a big part in net zero energy construction. Solar-powered energy systems, solar-powered roofing, and geothermal cooling and heating systems can all be vital elements in zero-energy buildings. Passive building techniques will also help homes be less dependent on outside energy sources. Making the most of a site’s natural wind, shade and light levels is already a booming trend in many European regions, and it’s expected to gain a lot of attention in the U.S. this year.

Designed for Drought

Water is a major concern in some regions of the U.S. Water-conscious building trends like gray-water recycling, storm-runoff reduction systems, drought-tolerant landscaping and indoor appliances that use less water will continue to rise in popularity as the cost of water usage also continues to rise. Gray-water and storm-runoff systems capture water that would otherwise go unused and make it available for non-potable use. Consumers benefit by getting "free water" to irrigate landscape. A move toward drought-tolerant and native landscaping not only saves water, but also saves homeowners money in landscaping supplies and labor.

Win-Win

Fortunately, when it comes to construction, what’s good for our budget and our health is also good for the environment. Some eco-conscious technologies and materials require a bigger original investment. But educating consumers on the personal benefits of green-building trends will help them understand the value of innovations that also conserve resources and protect the environment. Green construction is a trend that benefits everyone, including those who will be employed in the construction industry, as technological advances continue to support the resurgence of construction projects across the U.S.
