How to Get Your Interior Designer and Plumber to Communicate

By Chelsea Terris · 20.02.2013

Your client dreamed it up: the perfect bathroom or kitchen. Consulting with an interior designer to streamline her vision into a practical possibility is the first step. Because an interior designer is space specialist, she can help your client decide how to harmoniously organize your room: where to install cabinets, towel hangers, etc. However, when it comes to installing the pipes that make your sink, toilet, and shower run, communication with a trusty plumber is a must!

Working with two professionals who specialize in complementary but very different aspects of a job can pose a unique challenge. To make sure the process goes smoothly, review the following tips for facilitating clear communication between these necessary minds:

1. Conduct a few interviews: This initial step helps ensure that the duration will be pleasant and productive for all parties. Interviewing for the right fit will help your client avoid the hassle of having to hire again if one or both professionals don’t work well together or provide what she expects.

2. Be clear about goals, every step of the way: Unless your client wants to settle for a vision that is less than her original dream (note- less than, not different. Things change, but your client deserves to be totally satisfied!), make sure she is aware of and vocal about what she wants. If both your interior designer and your plumber hear input clearly, they will have an easier time delegating and getting the job done, to your specifications.

3. Learn to Speak Interior Designer . . . and Plumber: If you and your client understand what each professional is saying, you can both act as go-betweens if the two experience fallouts in communication. Using trade lingo with each person also allows them to take your client more seriously. Rather than investing in an industry-specific dictionary, advise your client to look at “history of” guides that include a few key terms. Terms like “Color Wheel” and “A’ Valve” are a must. When feng shui interferes with the location of base piping in a home, your client can join in the troubleshooting and keep both parties clear on their priorities.

4. Get Honest: If you or your client don’t like where the work is going, or how the interior designer and/or plumber work, individually or together, say so. If the issue is with both professionals, have a joint meeting so you can all get back on the same page.

5. Listen Well: Both the interior designer and the plumber have knowledge and insights that make them essential parts of your home project. Listen to what they have to say. If you and your client listen and learn, you both set a standard that allows for your specialists to do the same.

Coordinating with two specialists to create a beautiful space is both an inspiring process and hard work. By following the tips above, you as the performance contractor open up opportunities for each party to share ideas, learn something new, and contribute to a creative project that will stand the test of time.
