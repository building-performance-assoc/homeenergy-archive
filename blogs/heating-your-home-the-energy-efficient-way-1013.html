Heating Your Home the Energy Efficient Way

By Rix Petroleum · 08.06.2017

Heating your home can sometimes put a strain on your outgoings, especially during the colder months, and even more so during an unexpected cold snap. Whether you’re living in fear of receiving a large energy bill for turning the heating up, or you want to do your bit to stamp out large carbon footprints, these tips will show you how heating your home can be energy efficient as well as cost effective.

Insulating your loft will not only keep your home warmer for longer, you could also be looking to save up to £180 per year in heating bills. Loft insulation acts as a barrier for heat, keeping it trapped within your home and reducing your need to turn the heating up to feel the maximum benefits. Along with saving money on bills, loft insulation can reduce your carbon dioxide emissions by 730kg. Foam spray insulation is the method of applying insulation directly to the walls of your roof, which expands to fit and hardens when it sets. This method can also be up to 40% cheaper than traditional insulation methods.

As with loft insulation, your home can also lose het through its walls. Insulating your walls in a similar way can reduce your carbon emissions by almost 700kg as well as saving around £170 per year on heating costs. Opting for spray applied foam here has the added benefit of strengthening walls as it sets. As the spray foam expands to fill the space, it sets joint free, which means there are no gaps for warm air to escape through, making it the most effective method of insulation.

Heat from your home also escapes through the windows, so ensure you are equipped with double glazing to trap more heat. When window shopping, look out for specifications such as a gas between the panes of glass (typically argon or xenon) and keep an eye out for metal parts in the pane spaces – less is better! Well-designed double glazing can also save you £170 per year in energy bills as well as almost 700kg of carbon dioxide a year. To help with trapping heat in your home, invest in either blackout or thermally lined curtains. Not only will they keep daylight out, they will trap the heat in and prevent any draughts from creating a cold spell in your home.

Choosing the best source of heat for your home can make a difference to both your energy consumption and bills. Consider using heating oil as your main fuel source as it is generally the cheapest way to heat your home. On average, heating your home with domestic heating oil can save around £120 per year on your energy bills, compared to using mains gas.  It is also one of the most economical fuels on offer, as A-rated boilers can run at up to 97% efficiency when services regularly. The fuel requires storage space, typically in a garden, garage or shed, where it is housed in either a plastic or steel tank. Tanks bought from trusted suppliers have a lifespan of 15-20 years, with some suppliers giving a standard 10 year guarantee.

Heating your home doesn’t have to cost a fortune. By making some changes and upgrades to your home, you can start spending less on heating costs and improve your home’s energy efficiency at the same time. While some of these changes may seem fairly costly initially, they will quickly pay for themselves by saving you money on your energy bills.
