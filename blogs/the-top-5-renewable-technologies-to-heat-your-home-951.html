The Top 5 Renewable Technologies to Heat your Home

By Jude Knighty · 30.01.2017

Most people still continue to use fossil fuels to heat their homes, including gas fireplaces and electric heaters supplied by the utility companies who clearly value profit more than the environment. However, in recent years there has been a significant change in people’s mindsets and a huge shift towards renewable forms of energy to heat homes. Here are the major 5 ways you can generate heat for your home, with low-carbon, renewable technologies:

  1. Biomass or Wood Burning Stoves

Burning wood or biomass in the best pellet stoves has grown in popularity in recent years and since wood sources such as trees can be replanted it is considered a carbon-neutral practice. Waste from the construction industry is also commonly recycled to make wood pellets, as opposed to simply being dumped in landfills.

The benefits don’t stop there either, you can make huge savings by opting for biomass. For instance, if you are using an old electrical system, you could save more than $1000 a year. In environmental terms, it is much greener since carbon dioxide emissions are reduced by 15.7 tons a year when compared to coal fuel. In addition, you could save gain a $300 biomass tax credit.

  1. Solar Heating

Solar energy harnesses the energy from sun rays and new thermal systems use this free energy to heat water to create a source of hot water in your homes that can be used to heat radiators and other appliances. The great benefit of solar is that since sunlight is completely free, the only cost is the initial installation.

If you’re considering solar as a viable option, you will need to live in a moderately sunny climate and have roof space to install the panels. In addition, the panels should ideally be east to west facing to ensure it is exposed to the optimum levels of sun rays. 

  1. Air Source Heat Pumps

This is quite a new innovation that utilizes a pump, which in basic terms sucks heat from the outside air. This collected heat can then be used to heat the usual appliances including boilers, radiators and provide an efficient source of hot water. Even if you don’t necessarily live in a warm climate modern pumps can extract heat when the temperature is as low as -59° F.

If you are considering this option, you will need to make sure you have enough space for the system to have a good amount of airflow around it. In addition, this system is much more efficient if your property is well insulated to ensure the heat is not lost before it has time to heat your home.

  1. Thermal Stores

Who would have thought you could store excess heat, so it’s not just lost to the external environment? Well, you can thank this modern innovation. Although they work better when coupled with traditional wood biomass burning systems, they can also work with other systems including gas. These stores are typically composed of an insulated cylinder filled with water, often termed an accumulator tank.

 Heat stores can also provide a means of hot water and space heating for your home. However, bear in mind that thermal stores are typically combined with other forms of heating such as solar, wood burners and heat pumps. They are more of a means to increase the heating efficiency of your current heating technology, rather than a sole source of heat.

  1. Ground heat pumps

These pumps are installed deep into the ground and as the name suggests they conduct heat from the ground. This heat can be used all year round as the heat below the surface tends to keep at a constant level throughout the year. The heat collected can be utilized to heat radiators, boilers and water systems.

Since you’ll need to install the system via bore holes, the one thing you’ll need is some land. You don’t need a particularly large lot, but at the same time, it should be large enough to dig a trench and access below ground with ease. In addition, some jurisdictions may require planning permissions for this type of system.
