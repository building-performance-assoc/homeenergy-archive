Tiny House Competition

By Suzette Bienvenue · 09.07.2015

The Sacramento Municipal Utility District (SMUD) has created a first-of-its-kind energy-efficient housing competition for college and university students. The Tiny House Competition—developed by SMUD’s Energy & Technology Center and Community Solar program—includes twelve student teams from throughout California. The teams will design and build their own tiny houses and bring them to Sacramento State next fall for the competition. The winners will receive trophies and monetary awards.  

Why tiny houses?

SMUD is taking advantage of the teaching opportunity offered by the growth of the tiny house movement. The houses in this competition are being designed as zero-net-energy homes that will use solar power, efficient load management, and sustainable construction techniques.

The competition engages young people and challenges them to be creative in the search for ways to improve building performance and reduce costs. SMUD is also encouraging these students to help engage the community in conversations about renewable energy and energy efficiency. Many contemporary home buyers want to learn more about how to live sustainably, and this competition provides an exciting platform for those discussions.

How tiny are they?

Very. “Tiny houses” are considered to be any home with a footprint of less than 800 square feet. For this competition, the houses will be built on trailers with footprints between 100 and 400 square feet. One of the critical elements of the judging will be the effective use of space in the design.

How will the teams be judged?

The teams will be judged in four categories: Architecture, Home Life, Energy, and Communications.

  • Architecture judges will consider the design’s conceptual coherence and implementation by examining the design elements, effective use of space, and the overall consistency between architecture, construction, electrical, and mechanical systems.
  • Home Life judges will look at the house’s livability, marketability, and the fulfillment of the design specifications.
  • Energy judges will evaluate the construction specifications and team’s calculations of energy systems along with real world measured tests designed to replicate the target client living in the home.
  • Communication judges will consider each team’s messaging efforts via websites, printed media, web videos, and oral presentations for the judges and public tours.

How do these projects apply to the real world?

Each home will be designed around a specific end-user, meeting that user’s financial, social, and physical needs, as well as that of the user’s target environment. For example, a tiny house could be designed for a single, retired school teacher who plans to move to a rural community in the Sierra Nevada. Not only will the student team design and build a home for this hypothetical client, but it will also stage and landscape the lot at the competition site to represent the destination environment.

Where will the houses be displayed?

The Tiny House Village—a collection of all of the houses entered in the competition—will be open for public tours on Saturday, October 15, 2016. The exhibit will be located at Sacramento State on parking lot #9 at the south end of campus. Students will give tours of their homes and the public will learn more about tiny house construction while discovering the latest in energy-smart technologies. Vendors will also be available to showcase the use of renewable energy, smart appliances, efficient mechanical systems, and other ways to improve building performance. 

 

Suzette Bienvenue is an Education Specialist with SMUD’s Energy & Technology Center in Sacramento, CA. If you are interested in joining this event, please contact her.
