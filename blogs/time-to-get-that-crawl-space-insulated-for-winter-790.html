Time To Get That Crawl Space Insulated For Winter

By Richard Hale · 13.12.2015

For most of the country, we've seen a very mild winter without any deep cold Canadian arctic air pushing through. The weather pattern is finally changing and it looks like the back end of December is going to be much cooler for most of the country. I've been holding off getting to my crawl space insulation project due to weather, but it's time to get busy and so should you.

Crawl Space vs Basement

In the crawl space world, a basement is a basement and a crawl space is a crawl space. The difference? Basements are considered "living space" while "crawl spaces" are not. This is industry terminology. The other difference, basements have windows and crawl spaces have foundation vents. I go against this and you should to, you need to start treating your crawl space with a little more respect. Think basement insulation for your crawl space.

We want to keep warm air in, cold air out. We want to insulate between the inside and the outside of the home rather than insulating between the house in the crawl space. Install crawl space insulation on your foundation walls. This will give you best bang for your buck. 

Rigid Foam Or Spray Foam?

I know some of you may be thinking, should I use extruded rigid polystyrene insulation rather than spray foam polyurethane insulation. That's a great question. From my experience, you should use extruded rigid polystyrene insulation. Here's why. Spray foam absorbs a lot of water, which will lead you to issues that you don't want. The rigid foam is a much better option and absorbs far less water. Fiberglass insulation is not to be used.

Keeping Warm Air In

Think about this, if you're trying to keep heat in your home, you wouldn't open a window to let the cool air in, right? That makes no sense. Same thinking with your crawl space. To keep the crawl space at the right temperature, you have to keep the cold air out. Closing your foundation vents is simply not enough, they must be sealed up. The most cost effective way of keeping your crawl space sealed is closing those foundation vents.

Insulating Correctly Will Save You Big Money

When you're insulating your crawl space, you want to make sure it's done right. It can save you hundreds/thousands in energy cost. Remember the key insulating tips we discussed;

  • Insulate between the inside and the outside of the home rather than insulating between the house in the crawl space.
  • Use rigid foam insulation over other types.
  • Make sure foundation vents are closed
  • Seal your foundation vents.

Should I Keep The Crawl Space Sealed Year Round?

You have two options here, you can either take the seal off and open the foundation events in the spring or you can leave your crawl space sealed year round. If you do keep it sealed, you will have to condition the crawl space. This is also known as crawl space encapsulation.

Crawl space encapsulation is a generic term used to describe a crawl space that is protected from moisture by using a vapor barrier on the floor and walls. Moisture in the crawl space can lead to mold and odors, neither you want.
