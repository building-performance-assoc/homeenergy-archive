The Usual Suspects - Different Types of Asbestos in the House

By Northern Rivers Asbestos Removal · 02.11.2017









They’re mean, they’re ugly, and they’re lying in wait for unsuspecting homeowners, ready to jump out and infect you. What am I talking about? Types of asbestos, of course (if you said “my children”, I’m deeply sorry for them). Did you know there were six different types? Of course you did, but in reality, many people are unaware of the differences among these shifty minerals, and where they might be lurking.

Does my house have asbestos?

This wasn’t something I had given too much thought to, since my house can’t be more than 15 years old and was built after the numerous health concerns had been raised. Actually, though, asbestos has been found even in recently constructed homes, so it’s best to be on the safe side if you have any doubt whatsoever.

What is Asbestos?

Asbestos is actually a group of six types of naturally occurring minerals. While these minerals share many characteristics (odourless, tasteless and impossible to detect with the naked eye when it’s within a material) they have some differences too.


Chrysotile (White Asbestos)

The ringleader of the gang, and the most common of the different types of asbestos. Chrysotile is also known as white asbestos, or ‘serpentine asbestos’ thanks to its curly crystal fibres (or possibly because of its method of lying in wait for unwary homeowners). Historically, the vast majority of asbestos used in homes has been chrysotile, so it’s the prime suspect in any investigation. White asbestos can be found in walls, floors, roofs, ceilings and even insulation.



Amosite (Brown Asbestos) and Crocidolite (Blue Asbestos)

Used most often in pipe insulation, cement sheeting (amosite), spray-on coatings, plastics & cements (crocidolite), these are Chrysotile’s slightly unhinged but rarely seen cousins. Less common, they’re potentially more dangerous since less brown or blue asbestos exposure is needed to cause serious issues such as lung cancer. These, and the other three types, are known as amphibole asbestos.

The Rest of the Gang

Bringing up the rear are some of the less common types of asbestos, which are still a force to be reckoned with if you’re unlucky to come into contact with them. Luckily, the remaining three (anthophyllite, tremolite and actinolite) weren’t really used in any commercial settings, so the chances of you running into them in your own home are slim. Traces can still be found though, so they can’t be completely ruled out.

Be Safe

If you have to ask, “does my house have asbestos?” then you should probably consult a professional. Unless you’re 100% sure, it’s not worth putting your or your family’s safety at risk, even if the chance may be small. Luckily for me, after a quick sample was taken from various areas of my home, the tests came back negative, but I’m glad I found out without an invasive or inconvenient process. Your home might be different - are you willing to take the risk?
