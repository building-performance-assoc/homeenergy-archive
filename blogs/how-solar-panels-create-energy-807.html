How Solar Panels Create Energy

By Vince Macalister · 24.02.2016

How do solar panels create energy? To answer this question, it is best to start with a basic discussion about energy.

Basics of Electricity

If you took physics in high school, you probably remember learning about the conservation of energy: energy can neither be created nor destroyed. Given this fact, solar panels cannot really create energy, but they can convert it to another form. In this case, solar panels convert light into electricity.

Electricity is the flow of electrons within a material. At the atomic level, this occurs when electrons move from one atom to another. In some materials, this movement is rather difficult; in others, it is relatively easy. Materials in which electrons flow with ease are conductors or semi-conductors.

Different Methods of Producing Electricity

Now, there are several ways to produce electricity. Mechanical energy can be converted to electricity as with a turbine. When a conductor is moved in relation to a magnetic field, electrons flow. Whether the fuel source is nuclear, coal, natural gas, or something else, this is the main way most people power their homes and businesses. Likewise, chemical energy can be converted to electricity, as with a battery. In this case, a chemical reaction causes the flow of electrons.

Both the instances above are limited by the source of energy - the chemicals in the battery or the fuel that powers the turbine. Solar power, however, uses the light from the sun which is an essentially unlimited source of energy. The challenge is harnessing this source of energy to produce useful electricity. Solar panels achieves this through the photovoltaic effect.

Solar panels are constructed from two main materials – one rich in electrons and another deficient in electrons. This builds in the potential for electrons to flow. When sunlight hits the solar panel, some of the electrons in the electron rich material absorb the light’s energy and become excited. The excited electrons can break free from the original material and dissipate or flow to the electron deficient material, thereby producing electricity.

Advances in Solar Power

Since Becquerel discovered the photovoltaic effect in 1839, many scientists and organizations have been pushing the boundaries of solar power. Albert Einstein even won a Nobel Prize for his work in this field. Most of the applications, however, were limited to space exploration until the latter part of the 1900's.

In the latter part of the 20th century, companies began researching ways to provide affordable energy through solar. The two main challenges were to reduce cost and improve efficiency. Elliot Berman pioneered this field by exchanging efficiency for cost reduction. He found that it was cheaper to use silicon from several crystals rather than one, despite the loss in efficiency. This discovery opened up avenues for solar power in a few industries. Today, many new homes come standard with solar.

Benefits of Solar Power

These advances are truly exciting when you consider the potential benefits of solar power. It is no secret that solar power is one of the few renewable resources available. While it cannot yet replace the energy produced by other more traditional means, it can help to reduce them. Since fossil fuels are not renewable, solar power can help save them now, and perhaps eliminate their use in the future. Furthermore, solar power does not pollute the environment, cause loud noise, or require significant maintenance.

Perhaps the most important benefit to consumers, however, is the cost savings. While installation requires a higher upfront cost to a homeowner, the potential savings will outweigh the costs. On average, Arizona homeowners can save over $30,000 over 20 years with solar. Coupled with financing options, solar power looks like a better option every year. With the recent industry advances, it is only likely to improve.
