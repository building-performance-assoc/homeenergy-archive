The Energy Savings and Business Advantages of Tankless Water Heaters

By Jerry Callahan · 12.10.2015

Retail locations such as gas stations, banks and restaurants make up a substantial portion of the facility manager’s portfolio. Inside these traditional and often long-established commercial spaces, large tank-type water heaters are almost universal, creating a number of energy and businesses challenges.

Standard tank-type water heaters waste about 40 percent of the energy they consume through a process known as “standby loss.” These tanks, anticipating the need for hot water, must maintain the temperature of the water sitting in the tank while heat escapes through the tank walls. The water heater runs frequently to offset this standby loss, rather than satisfying actual demand.

Given the waste inherent in having hot water sit unused for hours as a time, facilities are looking at the benefits of tankless water heating technology. Tankless technology is particularly relevant in commercial settings where only periodic hot water usage is the norm. Tankless water heaters eliminate the problem of standby loss and provide hot water on demand.

Tankless devices don’t store hot water, but rather generate hot water instantly. They produce hot water on demand using either a powerful, conventional heating element, or Heatworks’ patented technology that converts electrical energy into heat. A tankless water heater can boost a flow of cold water to well over 100 degrees in just seconds.   

In addition, tankless electric water heaters can reduce energy usage compared to electric tank-type units because the temperature can be set lower. Tankless water heaters can be set at a temperature where no mixing with cold water is necessary in order achieve the desired temperature.

The costs associated with water heater failures highlights an advantage of a tankless design. Tank-type water heaters can leak or burst, causing a great deal of damage. Breaches that occur when a property is unoccupied can cause even more problems, as there is no one available to recognize the problem and shut off the water. Due to their design, a tankless water heater eliminates the risk of a flood in the facility and the prospect of devastating water damage.

The business advantages of a tankless water heater are also important considerations for the facility manager. In a commercial setting like a restaurant, space is at a premium. A traditional water heater with a 50 to 80 gallon capacity is typically around 68.5” tall and 26.75” in diameter. Tankless water heaters have a much smaller form factor that creates more usable square footage, freeing up an average of 12 to 24 cubic feet of usable design space, or over a 90 percent reduction in space consumption versus a storage tank. Moreover, tankless water heaters can be wall-mounted, completely eliminating the space considerations that come with a traditional tank-type water heater. 

Facility managers should also be aware of the water and energy savings that come from using tankless water heaters in a distributed water heating architecture. A distributed architecture locates water heaters closest to points of use, rather than in one centralized location. With tankless water heaters installed at points of use, there is a reduction in the amount of hot water stranded in the line when the faucet is turned off. In a centralized system, this stranded water, cooled to ambient temperature, must be pushed out of the way to deliver the hot water to the source. Likewise, whenever a user draws water from a tap in a centralized system, water is wasted during the wait for the pipes between the water heater and the fixture to fill with hot water.

With standard tank-type water heaters wasting a substantial amount of energy, facility managers can reduce energy expenses for their clients by switching to a tankless water heater. Clients will benefit not only from lower costs, but also from business enhancements such as more available space and the elimination of the risk of water damage.
