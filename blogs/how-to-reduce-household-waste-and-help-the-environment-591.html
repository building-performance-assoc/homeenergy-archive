How to Reduce Household Waste and Help the Environment

By Erich Lawson · 14.08.2014

There is a lot of debate going on about climate change and global warming. The naysayers claim it is a myth created by environmentalists and conservationists; while the supporters say it is a fact. Scientific research has shown the Polar icecaps melting at an alarming rate and changes in the weather patterns are visible world over. So, there must be some truth to climate change and global warming.

You may be wondering how this is linked to household waste. Well, the waste that you generate ends up in landfills, where it pollutes soil, air and water. It also causes vast stretches of valuable land to be filled with rubbish. The pollution is responsible for climate change and global warming. So, doesn’t it make sense to reduce the waste you generate and help the environment?

Tips to Reduce Household Waste

Here are some invaluable tips to help you reduce the amount of waste you generate. If you make these tips a part of your daily life, you will find they make a huge difference.

Buy What You Need: Usually people go to supermarkets and make impulse purchases. These buys are often useless and pointless. So create a shopping list and stick to it when you head out to the supermarket to buy your weekly groceries. The things that you rarely use or can borrow from family, friends and neighbors should be avoided.

Look to Reuse: When you buy something, always take a step back to see if you can reuse it. So instead of buying disposable batteries opt for rechargeable batteries and instead of using paper plates for parties, use your China. Also, learn to reuse bottles and jars to store knick-knacks; and paper and plastic bags can be reused for your grocery shopping. Look around your home and you will find there are several things, such as newspaper and envelopes, which can be reused effectively and sometimes, creatively.  


Use All-Purpose Cleaning Agent: Did you know household cleaning products are some of the biggest contributors to environment pollution? So, why use several cleaning products when a single, all-purpose cleaning agent can suffice? As far as possible, buy green and non-toxic products. Such products are ideal for homes with kids and pets and they do not pollute soil and water. Sometimes, using age-old cleaning methods, such as white vinegar, baking soda and lemon juice, turn out to be more effective and environment-friendly. Of course, let’s not forget they are cheaper as well.


Donate or Sell: Over the years every household accumulates unwanted items. These items end up in landfills majority of the times. You can save money, as you will have to pay for a skip and haulage if you dispose these things. Just look to donate items to local charities or friends, who can put them to good use. Alternatively, you can have a garage sale or even put out an ad online.

Take a second look at the things that you have kept aside as rubbish. You will find there are several things that you can reuse or give others. Even something like wood from packaging can be kept and you can take on a new hobby – making woodcraft.
