Does insulating paint work?

By Sam Mishriki · 16.06.2016

A fresh coat of paint can do a lot for a home or business. Among other things, it can be great for changing the way that a home looks and it can be good for protecting against different types of damage. When it comes to house painting, you may have paint salesmen or house painters that try to sell you on different kinds of paint that are alleged to have certain properties. One type of coating available for purchase are paints that are claimed to have insulating abilities. The selling point is with these products is that they add an additional layer of insulation to the home and help to cut the cost of heating and cooling.

What is insulating paint?

In short, insulating paints are paints that have additives that are supposed to provide the coat with insulating properties. In other words, the idea is to make a paint that can stop heat flow from one side to the other. If you look into insulating paints, you will find products that are intended for different purposes. Some are marketed for exterior painting, some are for roof painting and others may be sold as interior paints.

How insulation works

The basic idea behind all insulating materials is to prevent heat flow from one side of the material to the other. With many of the most effective forms of insulation, you have bulky materials that have a lot of room for air between the actual fibres of the material. This works because air is a poor conductor of heat. Essentially, the dead space reduces the ability of heat to travel from one side of the insulation to the other. To determine the value of a layer of insulation, you basically look at two factors: its ability to prevent heat flow and the thickness of the material.

Does insulating paint work?

You can make additives for paint that in theory will have insulating properties. However, paint falls victim to one of the components that will make for a good insulation – this crucial factor is the thickness of an insulating layer. Paint is applied in layers that are far too thin to have any significant effect in regard to insulation. Now, some of these products can be marketed as insulating paint because they might insulate at better levels than standard paint and there are no clear standards by which insulating paint is defined, but that does not mean that they act as an effective insulating layer.

Can paint play a role in insulating the home?

When it comes to the paints that are claimed to work as a layer of insulation, it is more of a gimmick than anything else, and it costs more than it could possibly be worth. However, paint can play a role in stopping heat gain, so it could be said that paints can play a role in the insulation of a home. Instead of looking for paints that make claims about insulating, a homeowner can look for paints that are more reflective. For exterior house painting and roof painting, you can use colours that reflect more of the light and heat, plus there are paints that have materials added that can reflect more of the heat away from the painted surface.

It would be great if paint could be used as an effective insulation, but the claims of companies that make these products often do not stand up to real world testing, and there are no independent studies that confirm the efficacy of any of these products. That is not to say that there will never be an insulating paint that is actually effective, but as of this writing, there are none that offer a worthwhile benefit.
