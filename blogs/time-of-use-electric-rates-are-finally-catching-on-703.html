Time-of Use Electric Rates are Finally Catching On

By Charlie Hewitt · 03.06.2015

In the home energy field, it is easy to focus our attention on efficiency improvements.  Whether it is relamping, installing home automation, or tightening the home thermal envelope, there are plenty of cool things to hold our attention. The problem is that some of these innovations will not deliver on their full potential without jazzing up the underlying electric rate structures. The good news is that behavior-based rate plans are becoming available for customers in both traditional utility and deregulated electricity markets.

Tried and true fixed price electricity plans offer peace of mind but the only savings opportunity is simply to use less electricity. Variable rate plans offer flexibility but can be tricky if the pricing formula is not transparent. In fact, Connecticut is set to ban residential variable rates after declaring they were potentially deceptive and predatory.  Fortunately, time-of-use plans are becoming more widely offered and accepted as an alternative to fixed and variable rates. 

Time-of use rates are not a new concept. Utilities have experimented with pilot programs for years. Real time usage data from smart meters combined with web-based communications and controls are allowing these rate plans to live up to their billing.  According to a customer behavior study analysis conducted by the Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory, financial benefits are the principal motivator for customers enrolling in time-of-use rates.

Time-of-Use Plan Types

Standard time-of-use plans charge higher rates for usage during peak demand times and lower rates when demand is low. Consumers can save money by shifting load. This includes running discretionary loads, like dishwashing, at night or saving up laundry for weekends. Other useful strategies involve using web-enabled thermostats or smart thermostats to reduce peak space conditioning load. Time-of-use plans also have a good tie-in with rooftop solar systems and plug-in electric vehicles.

Non-standard time-of-use plans include variable peak pricing, critical peak pricing, and critical peak rebates. Unlike standard time-of-use plans, these plans may not have set peak periods or prices. Variable peak pricing plans predefine peak usage periods but set rates during those periods based on market prices. With critical peak pricing plans, power grid and market conditions dictate the both the peak usage periods and prices. Typically, these plans are limited to a maximum number of hours during specific times of the day. 

Critical peak rebates are more of a voluntary curtailment product than a time-of use electricity rate. Customers can receive a bill credit for reducing load by a specified amount during a prescribed time interval when grid conditions create economic incentives to reduce load. Similar to critical peak pricing plans, these products are limited to a certain number of hours during specified times of the year.

Traditional Utility Service Areas

Arizona has some of the more established time-of-use rate plans in the country. Arizona Public Service offers several variations of standard time-of-use plans. Started as a pilot project in 1988, 52% of APS customers are now on time-of-use rates. Tucson Electric Power and the Salt River Project also have a significant number enrolled in time-of-use rates.

California electric utilities have conducted pilot projects with time-of-use rates. However, the California PUC is proposing the most significant reform to residential rates since that state’s energy crisis in 2000. Investor-owned utilities would be required develop time-of-use plans by 2019. These plans would become the default residential rates with customers having to opt-out rather than opt-in.

Duke Power has also launched a pilot time-of-use program in North Carolina. This traditional time-of-use plan separates usage into four pricing periods. In addition to peak and off-peak periods, there is an additional breakdown into summer and non-summer months. These are just a few examples showing a steadily growing acceptance of time variant plans available to customers served by regulated utilities.

Deregulated Electricity Markets 

Residential electricity consumers in deregulated markets are seeing more time-of-use rate plans offered from competitive suppliers. In Pennsylvania, PPL launched a new time-of-use program in January 2015. PPL lists several electric generation suppliers with residential time-of-use plans. These time-of-use plans are generally standard products where prices and usage periods known at the time of enrollment. One product reviewed featured one rate for weekday usage between 12 pm and 6:55 pm and another rate for all other times.

Texas, with its comprehensive installation of residential smart meters, is also seeing innovative time-based offers from electricity providers. These electric plans include free nights, free weekends, and other discounted usage periods. While these plans are competitive on their own, they can yield significant savings for customers able to shift load to the discounted or free usage period. 

Texas is also seeing offers for critical peak load reduction programs. A key element to increasing enrollment in these usage curtailment plans is that they are voluntary. There is no penalty if the consumer is unable to reduce their usage.

Time-of-use rate plans represent a huge opportunity for engaging consumers in meaningful load management. Utilities should support these rate plans by continuing smart meter deployment and building out their back offices to handle complex billing.  State regulators need to promote time-of-use rates by having utilities expand offerings beyond pilot programs. Rate plans with differing peak pricing periods may increase enrollments by allowing consumers to choose plans that match their ability to shift load.  Greater adoption of time-of-use rates will maximize the benefits of energy efficiency technologies and encourage further innovation in the home energy field.
