6 Steps to Write an Energy-Efficient Home Audit Report

By Freddie Tubbs · 16.05.2018

An energy-efficient home audit is a method the energy consumption of a person’s home is measured with, and an audit can help identify how energy conservation can be improved.

If you have never done one before or are looking for a way to streamline your energy auditing process, here are six simple tips to help you write up an audit report for an energy-efficient home.

1. Identify Air Leaks First

The first step to creating your audit report is identifying any air leaks in the house you are auditing. Drafts are hugely problematic and are one of the classic ways a home can go from being energy efficient to not being the one. Houses can save up to 20% per year by sealing drafts, and they can pop up in the most unexpected places. Gaps along flooring, underneath doors and worn out seals in windows are all places where air can easily escape from a house and reduce its overall efficiency.

2. Review Appliances & Lighting

Second to air leaks, inefficient lighting and appliances are another major contributor to a home not being energy efficient. “Given the fact that lighting accounts for around 10% of a household’s energy bills, using energy efficient lighting can drastically reduce these. Next up, check general appliances such as the refrigerator, washing machine and dishwasher - these appliances are used a lot and often left running constantly”, - explains Earl Allen, an Energy industry writer at Ukwritings and Paperfellows.

3. Write a House Plan

After you have figured out where the home you are auditing is losing energy, you can make a basic action plan by asking yourself a few simple questions. These questions will enable you to write up your audit summary and overall audit report.

  • How much money is being spent on energy?

  • Where is energy being lost?

  • How long would it take for the initial investment in energy efficiency to pay off for the homeowner?

  • Would making the home energy efficient provide additional benefits for the homeowner in addition to the money saving potential?

 

4. Summarise Your Findings

Your audit should consist of two parts: a concise summary and the detailed report. The concise summary should take no more than five minutes to read and give detailed facts about the findings of your audit. John Johnson, a Report writer at Boomessays says: “Your summary should get straight to the point: outline where and when your audit was conducted; the potential savings identified from the audit; and provide details of the household’s current utility bills”.

5. Start the Detailed Report

You will need to write up a detailed report which covers all the points and is easy to read. If writing is not your strong point, you may want to consider websites such as Academadvisor and State of Writing which provide grammar guides and information which can help you provide an accurate report.

In your detailed report, you should provide specific information about the home you carried out the audit on. This information should include details about the home’s overall square footage, the people residing within the home, the amount of energy currently being used by the home, any areas where energy is being wasted or lost, and information about anything else within the home which uses energy. The report should provide details about the load demand of appliances and the potential savings which could be made by improving the home’s overall energy efficiency.

6. Complete Energy Accounting Forms

Accounting forms, as you are probably aware, help track kilowatt usage and the energy which is used by each individual appliance. Energy accounting forms can help illustrate the demand which is being placed on energy by specific appliances or shortfalls in the home, such as drafts, and can help make decision makers come up with options for improving the home’s energy efficiency.

An energy-efficient home audit report needs to be completed when any homeowner is looking to improve their home’s overall efficiency. An audit report is a vital part in determining where shortfalls in energy efficiency are most prominent and help an action plan be drawn up to improve the home’s energy efficiency.
