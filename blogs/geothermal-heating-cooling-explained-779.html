Geothermal Heating & Cooling Explained

By Alex Smith · 09.11.2015

The air temperatures change with the seasons. In the northern hemisphere, you can count on heat in the summer and cold in the winter. And our heating, ventilating and air conditioning (HVAC) systems are designed to make our homes and other buildings more comfortable. These systems are powered by electricity, gas, propane, oil and other fuels.

However, while the air temperature is always changing, ground temperatures remain relatively stable — no matter the season. Geothermal HVAC systems take advantage of this stability to make our homes and other buildings more comfortable by relying on ground temperatures rather than fuel.

What Is Geothermal Heating and Cooling?

A geothermal heating and cooling system consists of three main parts:

  • Buried pipes collectively called an “earth loop”
  • A reinjection pump
  • The indoor unit

The installer can choose from different earth loop approaches. Most commonly, the earth loop’s pipes are buried to fit the site’s requirements. The pipes are meant to first circulate water up and through a heat exchanger. Then, the water is pumped back through a system called “reinjection.”

When it’s cold outside, the earth loop’s water grabs heat in the ground and carries it up and into your home. The indoor unit’s role is to compress this heat and bring it to even higher temperatures — and then circulate the heat through your home. When it’s warm outside, the earth loop works in reverse. It grabs the hot air from your home, and then carries it down and into the ground. The hot air is essentially dumped into the earth.

While there’s no gas, propane, oil or other fuel to burn, a geothermal heating and cooling system does use electricity to power its pump, fan and compressor. Even with the small amount of electricity it uses, a geothermal HVAC is a much cleaner, greener and efficient technology than traditional HVAC systems. Despite this technology’s innovative nature, it’s easy to use and requires little maintenance. In fact, geothermal heating and cooling systems should last longer than traditional HVAC systems.

The Geothermal Advantage

As green building practices become more prominent and widespread, so do geothermal heating and cooling systems. Progress is made each year, and it’s important that prospective geothermal HVAC owners have the right information as they decide what’s best for their homes. Here are just a few of the benefits to consider:

  • Efficiency — Yes, geothermal heating and cooling relies on electricity to power portions of the system. But the overall system is incredibly efficient and environmentally friendly.
  • Footprint — Some prospective geothermal HVAC owners expect the system to have a large footprint on their property. The truth is that geothermal heating and cooling systems are almost unnoticeable after installation. The earth loop is buried, and the entire system typically only takes up a few cubic feet.
  • Quiet — The whir of traditional HVAC systems is gone. A geothermal system runs quietly, and there’s no outdoor unit that sits on the side of the home or on the roof.
  • Longevity — A homeowner can expect to get much more usable life out of a geothermal system than a traditional. The earth loop lasts longest. Rarely will a homeowner have to dig up the old earth loop and replace it with a new one.
  • Versatility — There’s a myth that geothermal HVAC systems only heat. As demonstrated above, the system reverses its process when switching from heat mode to cool mode. Circumstances in different parts of the world may require backup heating or cooling systems, but most geothermal HVAC owners can depend on their systems for all climate control needs.
  • Breadth — Geothermal heating and cooling systems can extend beyond simply heating or cooling your home. They can be designed and installed to heat water and swimming pools, too, meaning you don’t have to install a second or third system for functions beyond climate control.
  • Conservation — What about water? If the earth loop uses water to carry the heat, does the entire system use a lot of water and increase costs? The short answer: No. Geothermal heating and cooling systems use an aquifer rather than your home’s water, and it’s recycled through the reinjection system.
  • Affordability — Government incentives help make geothermal heating and cooling more affordable and competitive with traditional units. Also, the long lifespan of a geothermal HVAC system makes it even more cost-effective once the initial investment period is past.

Is Geothermal Right for All Homeowners?

Different conditions and resources in different parts of the country will determine whether or not a geothermal heating and cooling system is feasible and cost-effective. Energy auditors, program managers, contractors and other professionals who develop an understanding of geothermal HVAC systems will be well-positioned to advise homeowners on whether or not geothermal heating and cooling is right for them.
