HVAC Tips for Energy Savings

By Dan McKee · 20.10.2016

Many households and businesses rely on heating, ventilation, and air conditioning (HVAC) all year round for optimal functioning. As these systems consume a considerable amount of power, discover energy saving approaches that could effectively decrease your annual power bill.

According to a report conducted by the US Department of Energy, space cooling single-handedly accounts for an average 15 per cent of the total electricity consumption in commercial buildings. In fact, more than 30 per cent of energy for powering HVAC systems goes to waste. Discover the following simple tips not only to save power, but also to maximize returns on investment.

Change HVAC filters regularly

When filters are clogged with dust and other foreign objects, they restrict proper flow of air, effectively causing HVAC systems to work overtime. While this leads to increased energy consumption, the quality of air sent indoors is compromised.

Further, you will incur huge costs in replacing the dysfunctional HVAC components. Let a skilled technician conduct regular checkups, such as changing filters, preferably on quarterly basis. Failure to do so will only lead to premature system failure.

Use programmable thermostats

Depending on the season, you can set the temperatures of your HVAC systems to lower or higher temperatures. This way, your business avoids unnecessary energy expenditure, for example, when some rooms are not in use.

Invest in thermostats that you can program as per the demands of your business. For example, a business that runs on a regular Monday to Friday schedule can take advantage of 5+2 day thermostat model.

If you spend considerable time away from home, say on holiday, you can program the machines to do less work while you are away. Let an experienced commercial technician help you to install the devices.

Proper maintenance and upgrade

Proper HVAC maintenance and upgrade can reduce total energy costs by 40 per cent. Just as a car needs regular tune-ups, your heating and ventilation systems require occasional servicing. Since they are machines, they are prone to faults and aging. For this reason, get an expert who is experienced in doing commercial HVAC maintenance.

Hiring an experienced technician is prudent, as he will use the right tools, equipment and materials or the tune-up. Conduct delicate vetting of local technicians and ensure the one you settle on is licensed and insured to do so.

Control direct sunlight through windows

If you experience high temperatures especially during the day, the inside of your building needs dedicated cooling. However, you can control the amount of sunlight entering your indoor space by using glass protectors, drapes, blinds, interior curtains, and solar films.

Additionally, consider growing trees and other vegetation on the side of the building through which sunlight enters. During winter, install southern windows to distribute the little heat from the outside to interior spaces.

Use fans

You can distribute the amount of humidity in the air using ordinary fans instead of relying on HVAC systems. While this makes the living space comfortable, it results to decreased energy consumption. Consider installing box fans nearer to the windows. They push humid air out, creating space for more comfortable air to seep in from the outside.

With proper use of fans, you avoid incurring huge power costs when the air conditioning machines are off.

Seal the ducts

You can increase the efficiency of HVAC systems by 20 per cent if you correctly seal cooling and heating ducts. Considerable energy is lost if the piping has faults, such as breakages and improper insulation.

Invest in simple materials, such as foil tape, mastic and duct sealants. Pay more attention to unprotected areas, such as crawlspaces, unheated storage spaces and underneath your building. Check signs of damage on the ducts. If the system is more than 10 years old, you could overhaul it for a new set of units.

Conclusion

Encourage every family member in your house and employees at your business premises to switch off lights when they are not needed. On the same vein, turn off or set all electrical appliances and equipment to sleeping mode when you don’t use them.

It is prudent to use the natural sunlight especially during the day. At the same time, remove traditional incandescent bulbs and replace them with energy-saving fluorescent lamps. These approaches will guarantee you lower power consumption and ultimately reduced power bills.
