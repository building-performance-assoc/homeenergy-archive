5 Simple Ways to Improve Thermal Efficiency at Your Home

By Ron Burg · 03.08.2016

There was a time when people had little to no concern about energy conservation at their home or offices. During those days, the demand for energy-saving electronic equipment or home fittings was far less than the demand that there is today. The incentive of saving a few extra bucks was not a big enough motivator to prompt people to make their homes more energy efficient.

A lot has changed since those days. People are more conscious about energy efficiency today than they have ever been, largely due to the widespread knowledge about dwindling energy resources and the potential threat of global climate change. Besides, the economy is not as stable as it once was before, which makes it imperative for homeowners to cut corners on their living costs and reduce their utility bills as much as they possibly can.

Reducing home energy costs is not as easy as it may sound. If you try to improve energy efficiency at your home without a solid plan, you may end up throwing away more money than you are doing right now. Heating and cooling are responsible for the majority of the energy consumption in the United States. Hence, it makes sense to boost the thermal efficiency of your home in an attempt to elevate its overall energy efficiency. Here are five simple ways you can make sure that your home is thermally efficient:

1) Energy Efficient Lighting

Lighting is one of the things that homeowners pay a lot of attention to when they are designing or decorating a particular house according to their own requirements. From now on, make sure that energy efficiency is a part of your requirement for lighting. Replacing old light bulbs with energy efficient bulbs is a very inexpensive way of making your home more thermally efficient. You may think that changing the lights cannot possibly make much of a difference, but in reality, it lowers your energy consumption significantly.

The lighting market offers a plethora of energy-efficient products in the form of fluorescent light bulbs that are designed to consume less energy, which have a longer lifespan, and which emit more light per wattage. In other words, you can lower the wattage of the bulbs without creating a dark and shady environment in your home.

2) Programmable Thermostats

Bringing home a programmable thermostat and getting it installed will have a massive impact on your overall energy consumption. The programmable thermostat is an inexpensive way of lowering your energy costs and enhancing your thermal efficiency. The value of the programmable thermostat lies in its ability to reduce your cooling and heating costs when you are away from home. It can do the exact same thing when you are asleep at night.

We are all well aware that there is no need to maintain a comfortable temperature all day long, especially when there is no one at home to enjoy that comfortable temperature. This gives rise to the creation of the programmable thermostat. The perk of having a programmable thermostat is that you do not have to manually turn on the system every time you enter your house. The system will be turned on automatically and will create a comfortable temperature at your home before you return.

3) Home Insulation

Insulating a house adequately is part and parcel of any endeavor that aims to bring down the energy costs at home. There are many ways you can go about insulating your house. Each type of insulation has its pros and cons. It does not matter what type of insulation you select, as long as you make sure that you are putting it to good use. You are strongly advised to always have more insulation than you need. What’s really interesting about insulation techniques is that they differ from one area to the other.

These techniques are based on weather conditions and the overall climate of a particular location. If your home is well insulated, hot and cold air will not be able to penetrate the walls of your house. As a result, the interior temperature will remain comfortable. By doing this, you can stretch every dollar that you are paying for heating and cooling at your home.

4) Repair Heat Exchangers

Heat exchangers are extremely important components of your air conditioning and refrigeration systems. Often these components can go out of order or malfunction horribly. When that happens, the heat transfer from one medium to the other is disrupted, and the overall thermal efficiency of the refrigeration or air conditioning unit goes down considerably.

With the thermal efficiency down, the energy consumption of your refrigeration and air conditioning unit will rise significantly, and you will end up paying a lot more for heating and cooling. This is why it is very important for the heat exchangers to undergo regular maintenance so that they can be repaired as soon as a problem arises. If the repair work is being carried out by a reputable service, you will not have to pay an exorbitant price for the maintenance.

5) Outdoor Light Sensors

Some people like to leave their porch lights and other outdoor lights on all night long. If you are one of those people, then you should consider replacing the ordinary outdoor lights with sensor lights. These are basically motion sensors that will make your outdoor lighting more flexible and give you far more options to explore. In other words, you can lower the cost of outdoor lighting without compromising the security that it provides.
