Fall 2017 Contributors

Published 01.08.2017

Kevin Beck

“Tiny House, Tiny Energy?”

Here is the takeaway from Kevin’s article: “Building a Tiny House for every climate and situation would be extremely difficult.  Proper planning, design, and construction are all crucial to ensure that your Tiny House is legal, livable, safe, and energy efficient.”

The most rewarding moments for Kevin are those when he’s teaching homeowners, fellow contractors, or designers about designing and building in a sustainable way, and “then seeing the lightbulbs go on when they get it.  At that moment, I know that he or she will never design or build in the same way again.”

What you didn’t know about him: Folks may be surprised to know that Kevin once lived in a 150 ft2 shed for eight months. “ I lived, ate, worked, and slept in this tiny space, and I enjoyed every minute!”

 

 

 

Kara Saul Rinaldi

“The Evolution of Smart-Home Performance”

“Home performance programs should find synergies with demand-response programs that utilize smart thermostats,” says Kara Saul Rinaldi. “A well-insulated and well-sealed home should be able to maintain its temperature during a demand-response event when the temperature is adjusted to limit strain on the grid.”

In December 2009, in Copenhagen, at the United National Conference on Climate Change, Kara cohosted a 20-person U.S. Congressional Delegation led by Speaker Pelosi and the chairs of most of the committees that touch climate change and energy. “I had such a remarkable time discussing the future of our planet and how energy policy and smart technology can reduce our carbon emissions with dedicated and passionate policy makers before they were whisked off to meet with President Obama.”

What you didn’t know about her: “When I was ten years old, I sent a letter to my newly elected Congresswoman Barbara Boxer telling her that I wanted to change policy too someday. Two weeks after graduating with my BA in political science from UC San Diego, I was working in then-Senator Boxer’s press office.

 

David Bohac

\"A New Way to Reduce Multifamily Air Leakage\"

“The aerosol sealing process provides incredibly large envelope leakage reductions—even for existing buildings,” says David Bohac, adding that he and other researchers “kind of expected the 81% leakage reduction for new construction, but were really impressed with the 78% reduction for five of the six units in the early 1900s masonry building.”

One Saturday in 1996, David says, “I woke up to see the newspaper headline ‘Some noise-insulated homes unsafe’—which was disturbing, considering that our organization ran that program.” But “with the help of Jim Fitzgerald and many others, we developed an automated combustion safety test and procedures to predict when tightened program houses would have IAQ issues.”

What you didn’t know about him: Most people don’t know that when David was in his early 20s, he and his wife road a tandem bike 4,500 miles across the United States and down the West Coast. “By the end of the trip we were averaging 90+ miles a day, and we hit over 55 mph on a long downhill in the Rocky Mountains.”

 

Theresa Gilbride

“DOE Zero Energy Ready Homes ”

“Just a few years ago, zero energy houses were rare; now, thanks to programs like DOE’s Zero Energy Ready Home program, these homes of the future are becoming available all around the country,” says Gilbride “Builders are proving that these high-performance homes are not pie in the sky but really pretty down to earth to build; and homeowners are finding that when you offset the monthly mortgage with energy bill savings, these homes can actually cost less than a code-built house from day one.”

The most memorable moments of Gilbride’s career take place at the Energy & Environmental Building Alliance national conference each year, where DOE presents its Housing Innovation Awards. She enjoys “walking into the conference room and getting to meet face to face with the many builders I’ve been talking with by phone and e-mail over the past several months. Some I’ve never met before, and some have partnered with the program for years and feel like old friends.”

What you didn’t know about her: \"While other girls were playing with dollhouses, I was building houses—tree houses, and doghouses, and birdhouses. I love building things! (Maybe this isn’t too surprising?)\"
